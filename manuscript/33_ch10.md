@chapter 10 | Eating for Your Symptoms
> Food can't make every symptom disappear, but small, targeted changes can take the edge off many of them.

This chapter is designed to be used as a reference. Each section focuses on a common symptom of the menopause transition and explains, in plain language, how food and daily habits may help, what may make things worse, and one or two simple actions to try. You will also find suggestions for recipes from Part Four that fit each symptom.

A few things to keep in mind:

- **Try one change at a time** for two to four weeks, and keep notes in your symptom tracker (Appendix A). That way you will know what actually helps you.
- **Triggers are personal.** Something that sets off hot flashes in one woman may make no difference to another.
- **Food works alongside other support,** not instead of it. If a symptom is significantly affecting your life, talk to your doctor about all your options (Chapter 6).

## Hot Flashes and Night Sweats

Hot flashes are caused by changes in the brain's temperature control center as estrogen fluctuates and falls (Chapter 2). Diet can't switch them off, but it can help reduce triggers and support your body overall.

### Common triggers (alcohol, caffeine, spicy food, large late meals) and cooling foods

Many women notice that certain foods and drinks make hot flashes more likely or more intense. The most common culprits are:

- **Alcohol**, especially red wine, and particularly in the evening
- **Caffeine**, in coffee, tea, cola, energy drinks and chocolate
- **Spicy foods**, such as chili, hot sauce and cayenne
- **Hot drinks and very hot foods**
- **Large meals**, especially late in the evening
- **Sugary foods and refined carbohydrates**, which can cause blood-sugar swings

What may help:

- **Eat regular, balanced meals** with protein, fiber and healthy fats to keep blood sugar steady.
- **Keep evening meals moderate,** and finish eating two to three hours before bed if night sweats disturb your sleep.
- **Stay well hydrated,** particularly after a hot flash or night sweat. Keep a bottle of cold water with you.
- **Choose cooling foods and drinks** when you feel warm: cucumber, watermelon, berries, salads, chilled soups, yogurt and iced herbal teas.
- **Include soy foods and ground flaxseed regularly.** Some women find that two to three servings of soy foods a day modestly reduce hot flashes over several weeks (emerging).
- **Reach and maintain a weight that feels healthy for you.** Some research suggests that weight loss in women with a higher body weight may reduce hot flashes, although this is not the case for everyone.

:::try
- Keep a "trigger diary" for two weeks. Note each hot flash and what you ate or drank in the hour before. Look for patterns.
- Swap your evening glass of wine for a Tart Cherry Evening Spritz or Cucumber-Mint Cooling Water on at least three nights.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Turmeric Tofu & Veggie Scramble, Sesame Edamame Noodle Bowl, Miso-Glazed Tofu Buddha Bowl, Ginger-Garlic Tofu & Vegetable Stir-Fry, Chili-Lime Edamame (go easy on the chili), Cucumber-Mint Cooling Water.

## Sleep

Poor sleep is one of the most common and exhausting parts of the transition. Night sweats, anxiety, needing the bathroom and lighter sleep all play a part. Your evening routine, including what and when you eat, can help.

### Evening eating, magnesium and tryptophan-rich foods, caffeine timing

- **Set a caffeine curfew.** Caffeine can stay in your system for many hours. Try to have your last caffeinated drink before about 2 p.m., and remember that green tea, black tea, cola and dark chocolate contain caffeine too.
- **Limit alcohol.** It may help you fall asleep, but it disrupts sleep later in the night and can trigger night sweats.
- **Get the timing right.** A very large meal close to bedtime can disrupt sleep, but going to bed hungry can too. Finish your main meal two to three hours before bed. If you need something later, choose a small snack.
- **Include tryptophan-rich foods.** Tryptophan is an amino acid the body uses to make serotonin and melatonin, which help regulate sleep. It is found in turkey, chicken, fish, eggs, dairy, tofu, oats, nuts and seeds. Eating it with some carbohydrate may help it reach the brain.
- **Get enough magnesium.** Magnesium plays a role in muscle relaxation and the nervous system. Pumpkin seeds, almonds, spinach, black beans and whole grains are good sources. Evidence for magnesium supplements and sleep is limited, so food is a good place to start.
- **Consider tart cherries.** Tart cherries contain small amounts of natural melatonin. A few small studies suggest tart cherry juice may modestly improve sleep in some adults (emerging). Choose unsweetened juice and keep portions small.
- **Watch late-evening fluids.** Drink well during the day, but taper off in the hour or two before bed to reduce night-time bathroom trips.

**Good bedtime snacks:** a small pot of Greek yogurt with a sprinkle of oats, a slice of whole-grain toast with almond butter, a few walnuts with a small banana, a warm mug of milk or soy milk, or a Golden Turmeric Latte made without coffee.

:::try
- Set a 2 p.m. caffeine curfew for one week and note any change in your sleep.
- Try a small, calming bedtime snack instead of going to bed hungry or overfull.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Baked Cinnamon Pear with Walnuts, Vanilla Chia Pudding Cups, Tart Cherry Evening Spritz, Golden Turmeric Latte, Greek Turkey Meatballs with Tzatziki, Three-Bean Vegetable Chili.

## Mood and Anxiety

Changes in mood are very common during the transition (Chapter 5). While food can't replace professional support when it is needed, it can make a meaningful difference to how resilient you feel day to day.

### Blood-sugar balance, omega-3s, the gut-brain axis

- **Keep blood sugar steady.** Long gaps between meals, or meals based mainly on refined carbohydrates, can lead to blood-sugar dips that show up as irritability, anxiety and low mood. Eat regular meals that combine protein, fiber and healthy fats.
- **Eat oily fish twice a week.** Omega-3 fats, particularly EPA, are important for brain health, and some studies suggest they may help with depressive symptoms (emerging). Plant sources include walnuts, chia seeds and ground flaxseed.
- **Look after your gut.** The gut and brain communicate constantly through nerves, hormones and the immune system, a connection known as the **gut-brain axis**. Most of the body's serotonin is actually made in the gut. A varied, fiber-rich diet with fermented foods supports a healthy gut microbiome, which may in turn support mood (emerging).
- **Follow a Mediterranean-style pattern.** Several studies have linked Mediterranean-style eating with a lower risk of depression, and one well-known trial found that improving diet quality helped symptoms in people with depression (emerging).
- **Get enough folate, B12 and vitamin D.** Low levels of these nutrients have been linked to low mood.
- **Be mindful of alcohol and caffeine.** Alcohol is a depressant, and too much caffeine can heighten anxiety and trigger palpitations.

:::try
- Make sure you never go more than four to five hours without eating during the day this week.
- Add one portion of oily fish, or a tablespoon of ground flaxseed or chia seeds, to your meals on three days.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Sheet-Pan Salmon with Broccoli & Sweet Potato, Smoked Salmon & Avocado Rye Toast, Golden Flax & Berry Overnight Oats, Walnut-Date Energy Bites, Kefir Berry Shake.

## Energy and Fatigue

Feeling drained is one of the most common complaints in midlife. Poor sleep, stress, heavy periods and changes in blood sugar all play a part. If you feel exhausted all the time, see your doctor to rule out conditions such as anemia, thyroid problems, low vitamin D or B12, diabetes and depression.

### Iron, B12, protein at breakfast, hydration

- **Start the day with protein.** A breakfast with around 25 to 30 grams of protein helps keep energy steady through the morning and reduces mid-morning cravings.
- **Check your iron.** If your periods are heavy, you may be low in iron even if you are not yet anemic. Ask your doctor to check your ferritin. Include iron-rich foods such as lean red meat, chicken, fish, lentils, beans, tofu and fortified cereals, and pair plant sources with vitamin C to help absorption.
- **Think about B12.** Absorption declines with age and with some medications. Vegans need a reliable source, such as fortified foods or a supplement.
- **Drink enough fluid.** Even mild dehydration can make you feel tired and foggy.
- **Choose slow-release carbohydrates.** Whole grains, beans, lentils and starchy vegetables provide steady energy compared with white bread, pastries and sweets.
- **Move.** It may feel counterintuitive, but regular activity is one of the most effective ways to boost energy.

:::try
- Have a protein-rich breakfast every day this week.
- Fill a water bottle each morning and aim to finish it by mid-afternoon.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Spinach & Feta Egg Muffins, Besan Chilla with Mint Yogurt, Green Glow Protein Smoothie, Spiced Red Lentil Dal with Spinach, Lentil Bolognese with Whole-Grain Pasta.

## Cravings and Emotional Eating

Many women notice stronger cravings during the transition, especially for sweet or starchy foods in the afternoon and evening. This is not a lack of willpower.

### Why they happen and kind strategies

Cravings can be driven by:

- **Blood-sugar dips** after meals that are low in protein and fiber
- **Poor sleep**, which increases the hunger hormone ghrelin and decreases the fullness hormone leptin
- **Stress**, through the effects of cortisol on appetite
- **Hormonal shifts**, including premenstrual changes in perimenopause
- **Restriction**: skipping meals or banning certain foods often makes cravings stronger
- **Emotions**: boredom, loneliness, sadness, exhaustion or the need for comfort

Kind, practical strategies:

- **Eat regular, satisfying meals.** Most cravings are much easier to manage when you are not overly hungry.
- **Build in a planned snack.** If you always crave something sweet at 4 p.m., plan for it: an apple with almond butter, a few squares of dark chocolate with almonds, or a yogurt bowl.
- **Pause before eating.** Ask yourself: "Am I physically hungry, or is something else going on?" If you are not hungry, what do you really need? Rest, a break, connection, a cry, fresh air?
- **Allow all foods.** Labeling foods as "forbidden" often makes them more tempting. Enjoy treats mindfully, without guilt, and savor them.
- **Make your environment work for you.** Keep satisfying snacks easy to reach, and less nutritious foods a little less convenient.
- **Prioritize sleep.** It is one of the most powerful tools for reducing cravings.

If emotional eating feels out of control, or food and eating cause you significant distress, please talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian. You deserve support.

:::try
- Plan your afternoon snack in advance on five days this week.
- When a craving strikes, wait ten minutes and drink a glass of water. If you still want the food, enjoy it slowly and without guilt.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Dark Chocolate & Almond Clusters, Apple Slices with Almond Butter & Cinnamon, Berry Greek Yogurt Bark, Banana-Oat Mini Muffins, Spiced Pumpkin Seed Trail Mix.

## Brain Fog

Forgetfulness, difficulty concentrating and losing your train of thought are very common during perimenopause (Chapter 5). They usually improve with time, and good nutrition supports your brain along the way.

### Omega-3s, steady blood sugar, hydration and sleep

- **Omega-3 fats** are a major structural part of the brain. Eat oily fish twice a week, or include walnuts, chia seeds and ground flaxseed daily.
- **Steady blood sugar** helps your brain, which relies on a constant supply of glucose. Regular balanced meals help prevent the dips that worsen concentration.
- **Hydration** matters: even mild dehydration can affect attention and memory.
- **Berries and leafy greens** are rich in protective plant compounds. The MIND diet, a combination of the Mediterranean and DASH diets that emphasizes these foods, has been linked with slower cognitive decline in observational studies (emerging).
- **Sleep** is when your brain clears waste and consolidates memories. Protecting your sleep is one of the best things you can do for brain fog.

:::try
- Add a handful of berries and a portion of leafy greens to your day, every day this week.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Golden Flax & Berry Overnight Oats, Mediterranean Lentil & Roasted Vegetable Salad, Tuna & White Bean Niçoise Salad, Sheet-Pan Salmon with Broccoli & Sweet Potato, Frozen Yogurt-Dipped Blueberries.

## Bloating and Digestion

Fluctuating hormones affect the gut, so bloating, gas, constipation and a more sensitive stomach are common during the transition. Stress and changes in eating patterns can add to this.

### Pacing fiber, fermented foods and common trigger foods

- **Increase fiber gradually.** Fiber is essential for digestion, but adding a lot at once can cause bloating. Increase slowly over a few weeks, and drink plenty of water to help fiber do its job.
- **Include fermented foods.** Yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, miso and tempeh contain live bacteria that may support gut health. Start with small amounts if you are not used to them.
- **Eat slowly and chew well.** Eating quickly means swallowing more air.
- **Move after meals.** A short walk helps digestion.
- **Notice common triggers.** Carbonated drinks, chewing gum, sugar alcohols (such as sorbitol and xylitol, found in "sugar-free" products), very fatty meals, large amounts of beans or cruciferous vegetables (if you are not used to them), alcohol and caffeine can all cause bloating in some people.
- **Consider whether a particular food group bothers you.** Some people are sensitive to certain fermentable carbohydrates known as FODMAPs (found in foods such as onion, garlic, wheat, some fruits and legumes). If you suspect this, work with a dietitian rather than cutting out lots of foods on your own.
- **Drink enough fluid** to help prevent constipation.

:::note When to see a doctor
See your doctor if bloating is persistent (most days for three weeks or more), especially if you also have a loss of appetite, feel full quickly, have pain in your abdomen or pelvis, need to urinate more often, or notice changes in your bowel habits, unexplained weight loss or bleeding. These symptoms are usually caused by something harmless, but persistent bloating can occasionally be a sign of ovarian or bowel cancer and should always be checked.
:::

:::try
- Add one small serving of a fermented food each day this week.
- Take a 10-minute walk after your evening meal.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Ginger Brown Rice Congee with Soft Egg, Greek Yogurt Power Bowl, Miso-Glazed Tofu Buddha Bowl, Kefir Berry Shake, Golden Turmeric Latte.

## Joint Aches and Stiffness

Estrogen helps reduce inflammation and maintain the tissues around your joints. As levels fall, many women experience new aches and stiffness, particularly in the morning.

### Anti-inflammatory foods, omega-3s, vitamin D and gentle movement

- **Follow an anti-inflammatory eating pattern** (Chapter 9), rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains, beans, nuts, seeds, olive oil and oily fish.
- **Include omega-3 fats** from oily fish, walnuts, flaxseed and chia seeds.
- **Use spices such as turmeric and ginger.** These contain natural compounds with anti-inflammatory properties. Research on turmeric (curcumin) supplements for joint pain is promising (emerging), but supplements can interact with some medicines, including blood thinners, so check with your doctor first. Using these spices generously in cooking is a safe, delicious start.
- **Check your vitamin D.** Low vitamin D is common and can contribute to aches and muscle weakness.
- **Keep moving.** Gentle, regular movement, such as walking, swimming, cycling, yoga and strength training, helps keep joints mobile and builds the muscles that support them.
- **Maintain a weight that feels healthy for you.** Every extra pound of body weight adds several pounds of pressure on your knees when you walk, so even small changes can ease the load.

:::try
- Cook with turmeric, ginger or both at least three times this week.
- Start each morning with five minutes of gentle stretching from the Pose Library in Part Six.
:::

**Recipes to try:** Turmeric Tofu & Veggie Scramble, Coconut Chickpea & Vegetable Curry, Spiced Red Lentil Dal with Spinach, Baked Cod with Tomatoes, Olives & Capers, Golden Turmeric Latte.

## Symptom-to-Food Quick-Reference Table

| Symptom | Foods and habits that may help | Possible triggers to notice |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot flashes and night sweats** | Regular balanced meals, soy foods, flaxseed, cold water, cooling foods | Alcohol, caffeine, spicy food, hot drinks, large late meals |
| **Sleep** | Tryptophan-rich foods, magnesium-rich foods, small bedtime snack, tart cherries | Caffeine after 2 p.m., alcohol, large or spicy late meals |
| **Mood and anxiety** | Steady blood sugar, oily fish, fiber and fermented foods, Mediterranean pattern | Skipped meals, alcohol, too much caffeine |
| **Energy and fatigue** | Protein at breakfast, iron-rich foods with vitamin C, B12, fluids, whole grains | Sugary snacks, skipped meals, dehydration |
| **Cravings** | Regular meals with protein and fiber, planned snacks, good sleep | Restriction, long gaps between meals, poor sleep, stress |
| **Brain fog** | Omega-3s, berries, leafy greens, steady blood sugar, fluids | Dehydration, poor sleep, blood-sugar dips |
| **Bloating and digestion** | Gradual fiber increase, fermented foods, fluids, walking after meals | Fizzy drinks, sugar alcohols, eating quickly, sudden fiber increases |
| **Joint aches** | Anti-inflammatory foods, omega-3s, turmeric, ginger, vitamin D, movement | Highly processed foods, inactivity |

:::summary
- Food can't switch off symptoms, but targeted changes can reduce triggers and support your body.
- Test one change at a time for a few weeks, and track what works for you.
- For hot flashes, notice triggers such as alcohol, caffeine, spicy food and large late meals.
- For sleep, set a caffeine curfew, limit alcohol and get your meal timing right.
- For mood, energy, cravings and brain fog, steady blood sugar is key: eat regular meals with protein, fiber and healthy fats.
- For bloating, increase fiber gradually and include fermented foods.
- For joints, lean into anti-inflammatory foods, omega-3s and gentle movement.
:::
