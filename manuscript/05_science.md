@front A Note on the Science
Menopause research has grown enormously in the past twenty years, and it's still moving fast. Some recommendations rest on large, well-designed studies that have been repeated many times. Others are based on promising early findings that need more work before anyone can be confident. A few popular claims have very little evidence behind them at all.

Throughout this book I've tried to be honest about which is which. Where it matters, you'll see two labels. **Established** means the advice is backed by strong, consistent evidence, such as large clinical trials, systematic reviews or the guidelines of major health organizations, and you can act on it with confidence. **Emerging** means the research is early or mixed: interesting, and possibly worth trying if it's safe for you, but not settled.

## Where the Information Comes From

The guidance here draws on the published recommendations of The Menopause Society (formerly the North American Menopause Society), the UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), the National Health Service (NHS), the US National Institutes of Health (NIH), the World Health Organization (WHO) and leading bone, heart and cancer research organizations, as well as peer-reviewed studies. You'll find references and trusted resources at the back of the book.

Science deals in averages, and you're an individual. A change that helps most women may do nothing for you, and something with only emerging evidence may make a real difference in your life. Stay curious, try one change at a time, notice how you feel, and talk to a healthcare professional about anything that affects your medical care. If something here differs from what your doctor tells you, trust the person who knows your history, and feel free to bring this book to your next appointment.

## About the Women in These Pages

Each chapter opens with a short story about a woman going through the transition. These women are composites. Their stories are drawn from experiences that are common in research, in support groups and in conversations with friends, but the names, ages and details are invented, and none of them describes a real, identifiable person. They come from different backgrounds, cultures and stages of life, because menopause looks different for every woman, and I wanted more of you to recognize yourselves here.
