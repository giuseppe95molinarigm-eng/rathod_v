@front A Note on the Science
Menopause research has grown enormously in the past twenty years, and it is still evolving. Some recommendations rest on large, well-designed studies that have been repeated many times. Others are based on promising early findings that need more research before we can be confident. A few popular claims have very little evidence behind them at all.

Throughout this book, we have tried to be honest about which is which. Where it matters, you will see two simple labels:

- **Established:** supported by strong, consistent evidence, such as large clinical trials, systematic reviews or the guidelines of major health organizations. You can feel confident acting on these.
- **Emerging:** supported by early or mixed research. These ideas are interesting and may be worth trying if they are safe for you, but the evidence is not yet settled.

## Where Our Information Comes From

The guidance in this book draws on the published recommendations of respected organizations, including The Menopause Society (formerly the North American Menopause Society), the UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), the National Health Service (NHS), the US National Institutes of Health (NIH), the World Health Organization (WHO) and leading bone, heart and cancer research organizations, as well as peer-reviewed scientific studies. A list of references and trusted resources appears at the back of the book.

## What This Means for You

Science gives us averages. You are an individual. A change that helps most women may not help you, and something with only emerging evidence may make a real difference in your life. The best approach is to stay curious, try one change at a time, notice how you feel, and talk to a healthcare professional about anything that affects your medical care.

Health advice also changes as new research appears. If you read something here that differs from what your doctor tells you, trust the person who knows your history, and feel free to bring this book along to your next appointment.
