## Three Questions

1. **Have you gone 12 months or more without a period** (with no other explanation, such as pregnancy, a hormonal IUD or certain medicines)?
   - Yes: you're **post-menopausal**.
   - No: go to question 2.
2. **Have your periods changed** in timing, flow or frequency, or have you started noticing hot flashes, night sweats, broken sleep or new anxiety?
   - Yes: you're probably in **perimenopause**.
   - No: you may not have started the transition yet, and the habits in this book will give you a great head start.
3. **Have you had a hysterectomy or your ovaries removed, or do you use a hormonal IUD?** Then periods can't guide you. Use your symptoms and age instead, and talk to your doctor. If both ovaries were removed, you're in surgical menopause.

In this book, the year or so either side of your final period is often called the "menopause" stage, because that's when many symptoms peak. Don't worry about labeling yourself precisely. The stages blend into one another, and the core of eating well stays the same throughout. Only the emphasis shifts.

## Your Stage on One Page

| | Perimenopause | Around menopause | Post-menopause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main focus** | Steady energy, mood, sleep and cycle changes | Hot flashes, sleep, joints, body composition | Long-term bone, heart, muscle and brain health |
| **Priority nutrients** | Iron (if periods are heavy), B vitamins, magnesium, protein, fiber | Protein, calcium, vitamin D, fiber, phytoestrogens | Protein, calcium, vitamin D, vitamin B12, omega-3s |
| **Symptoms to target** | Energy dips, cravings, anxiety, PMS, bloating | Hot flashes, night sweats, sleep, joint aches | Lingering hot flashes, joint stiffness, dry skin |
| **Foods to lean on** | Lentils, beans, leafy greens, lean meat or tofu, whole grains, nuts and seeds | Soy foods, flaxseed, dairy or fortified alternatives, oily fish, colorful vegetables | Oily fish, dairy or fortified alternatives, eggs, legumes, berries, nuts, olive oil |
| **Iron needs** | 18 mg a day (ages 19 to 50) | 18 mg to age 50; 8 mg from 51 | 8 mg a day (from 51) |
| **Calcium needs** | Around 1,000 mg a day | Around 1,200 mg a day from age 51 | Around 1,200 mg a day |

## When to Eat and Drink

The rhythm that suits most women at every stage is simple: **three balanced meals a day, plus a snack if you need one.** Regular meals keep blood sugar steady, calm cravings and support better energy and mood.

In **perimenopause,** don't skip breakfast if you tend to crash mid-morning, and if premenstrual cravings hit, plan a satisfying late-afternoon snack rather than relying on willpower. **Around menopause,** keep evening meals moderate and finish eating two to three hours before bed if night sweats are a problem. Notice, too, whether hot drinks, spicy food or alcohol in the evening set off hot flashes. In **post-menopause,** make sure every meal carries a good portion of protein, around 20 to 30 grams, to protect muscle; appetite can shrink with age, so make each bite count.

Hydration matters at every stage, and even more if hot flashes and night sweats leave you damp. The US National Academies suggest women need about 2.7 liters (around 11 cups) of total water a day from all drinks and foods, with roughly a fifth usually coming from food. In practice, aim for six to eight glasses of fluid a day, more in hot weather or when you exercise. Water, milk, herbal tea, and even tea and coffee all count. Pale yellow urine is a good sign you're drinking enough.
