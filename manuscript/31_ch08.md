@chapter 8 | Not Sure of Your Stage? Start Here
> Wherever you are in the transition, there is a simple place to begin.

Welcome to the nutrition part of the book. If you have read Part One, you will already have a good sense of where you are in the menopause transition. If you have jumped straight here, that's fine too. This short chapter will help you work out your stage and point you to the parts of the book that will help you most.

## Which Stage Am I In?

Answer these three questions:

1. **Have you gone 12 months or more without a period** (without another explanation, such as pregnancy, a hormonal IUD or certain medications)?
   - Yes: you are **post-menopausal**.
   - No: go to question 2.
2. **Have your periods changed** in timing, flow or frequency, or have you started to notice symptoms such as hot flashes, night sweats, disrupted sleep or new anxiety?
   - Yes: you are probably in **perimenopause**.
   - No: you may not have started the transition yet. The habits in this book will still give you a great foundation.
3. **Have you had a hysterectomy, use a hormonal IUD, or had your ovaries removed?** It may be harder to tell your stage from periods alone. Use your symptoms and age as your guide, and talk to your doctor. If both ovaries have been removed, you are in surgical menopause.

The year or so either side of your final period is often called the "menopause" stage in this book, because many symptoms peak around this time. Don't worry about labeling yourself exactly. The stages blend into one another, and the core principles of eating well are the same throughout.

## Your Stage at a Glance

| | Perimenopause | Around menopause | Post-menopause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main focus** | Steady energy, mood, sleep and cycle changes | Hot flashes, sleep, joints, body composition | Long-term bone, heart, muscle and brain health |
| **Priority nutrients** | Iron (if periods are heavy), B vitamins, magnesium, protein, fiber | Protein, calcium, vitamin D, fiber, phytoestrogens | Protein, calcium, vitamin D, vitamin B12, omega-3s |
| **Common symptoms to target** | Energy dips, cravings, anxiety, PMS, bloating | Hot flashes, night sweats, sleep, joint aches | Lingering hot flashes, joint stiffness, dry skin |
| **Foods to emphasize** | Lentils, beans, leafy greens, lean meat or tofu, whole grains, nuts and seeds | Soy foods, flaxseed, dairy or fortified alternatives, oily fish, colorful vegetables | Oily fish, dairy or fortified alternatives, eggs, legumes, berries, nuts, olive oil |
| **Iron needs** | Around 18 mg a day | Around 18 mg until periods stop | Around 8 mg a day |
| **Calcium needs** | Around 1,000 mg a day | Around 1,200 mg a day from age 51 | Around 1,200 mg a day |

## Meal Timing and Hydration at Each Stage

The basic rhythm that suits most women at every stage is simple: **three balanced meals a day, with a snack if you need one.** Regular meals help keep blood sugar steady, reduce cravings and support better energy and mood. A few stage-specific tips:

- **Perimenopause.** Don't skip breakfast if you tend to crash mid-morning. Include protein at every meal to steady blood sugar. If you have premenstrual cravings, plan a satisfying snack for the late afternoon rather than relying on willpower.
- **Around menopause.** Keep evening meals moderate in size and finish eating two to three hours before bed if night sweats are a problem. Notice whether hot drinks, spicy food or alcohol in the evening trigger hot flashes.
- **Post-menopause.** Make sure each meal contains a good portion of protein (around 25 to 30 grams) to protect muscle. Appetite can decrease with age, so if you are eating less, make every bite nutritious.

Hydration matters at every stage, and even more so if hot flashes and night sweats make you lose fluid. The US National Academies suggest that women need around 2.7 liters (about 11 cups) of total water a day from all drinks and foods, with roughly a fifth usually coming from food. In practice, aim for six to eight glasses of fluid a day, more in hot weather or when you exercise. Water, milk, herbal teas and even tea and coffee all count. Pale yellow urine is a good sign that you are drinking enough.
