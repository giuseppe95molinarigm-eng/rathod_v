@chapter 14 | Lunches
> The afternoon slump is partly your body clock. What's in your lunchbox can make it deeper or shallower.

%% It's Grace's first week back at the office, six weeks after the surgery that removed her ovaries. She's forty-one. The genetic test said BRCA1, and she didn't hesitate, but nobody warned her how suddenly menopause would arrive: hot flashes within days of coming home, broken nights ever since. At 12:30 her team heads for the food court. Grace opens the container she packed last night: lentil salad, roasted peppers, feta, a scatter of seeds. "Look at you," says Marcus from accounts, "so organized." She laughs. What she doesn't say is that this lunch is one of the few things this month that has gone exactly to plan. At three o'clock, when the slump comes, she'll still be standing.

A good lunch carries you through the afternoon without the slump. These recipes are built on Anchor, Add, Adapt: a generous portion of protein, plenty of plants, a fiber-rich carbohydrate and a little healthy fat. Most of them pack well, keep for several days in the fridge and work just as happily as a light dinner. Many also fit the "cook once, eat three times" approach, so a pot of soup, dal or grain salad on Sunday can cover lunch for much of the week. Look for the **Make-Ahead** label and the storage notes in each recipe.

:::note Packing tips
- Keep dressings and crunchy toppings in a small separate container and add them just before eating.
- Pack soups and dal piping hot in an insulated food flask to keep them warm until lunchtime.
- Add a piece of fruit and a small handful of nuts if you have a long afternoon ahead.
:::

@recipe Mediterranean Lentil & Roasted Vegetable Salad
@tags GF | V | DF option | VG option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 30 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈20 g
This is the salad to make when you know the week is going to be busy. It actually gets better after a night in the fridge, as the lentils soak up the lemony, garlicky dressing. Charred peppers and sweet roasted tomatoes, salty feta and olives, a crunch of pumpkin seeds: it's a lunch that makes your colleagues ask what you're eating.

### Ingredients

* 1 medium zucchini, cut into chunks
* 1 red bell pepper, cut into chunks
* 1 small red onion, cut into wedges
* 1 1/2 cups (225 g) cherry tomatoes
* 2 tbsp olive oil
* 1 tsp dried oregano
* 2 cans (15 oz / 400 g each) green or brown lentils, drained and rinsed (or 3 cups / 600 g cooked)
* 1 can (15 oz / 400 g) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 2 large handfuls (60 g) arugula or baby spinach
* 1/2 cup (75 g) crumbled feta cheese
* 1/4 cup (35 g) pitted Kalamata olives, halved
* 2 tbsp toasted pumpkin seeds

#### For the dressing

* 3 tbsp olive oil
* Juice of 1 lemon
* 1 tsp Dijon mustard
* 1 small garlic clove, grated
* 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley or mint
* Salt and black pepper

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Spread the zucchini, pepper, onion and tomatoes on a large baking tray. Toss with the olive oil, oregano, a pinch of salt and pepper.
2. Roast for 25 to 30 minutes, turning once, until tender and lightly charred.
3. Meanwhile, whisk all the dressing ingredients together in a large bowl.
4. Add the lentils and chickpeas to the dressing and toss well.
5. Add the roasted vegetables and arugula and toss gently.
6. Scatter over the feta, olives and pumpkin seeds. Serve warm or at room temperature.

:::swaps
- **Dairy-free / vegan:** leave out the feta, or use a dairy-free alternative, and add cubes of baked tofu or extra chickpeas for protein.
- **More protein:** top with flaked salmon, grilled chicken or a boiled egg.
- **Grains:** stir in 1 cup (185 g) cooked quinoa or farro (farro isn't gluten-free).
- **Storage:** keep the arugula separate if making ahead, and add just before eating.
:::

:::why
Lentils provide plant protein, fiber, folate and iron. The colorful vegetables bring antioxidants, olive oil adds heart-healthy fats, and feta contributes calcium.
:::

@recipe Sesame Edamame Noodle Bowl
@tags DF | VG | GF option | 30 min | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 15 min | Cook 10 min | Keeps 3 days | Protein ≈25 g
Cold noodles might sound like an odd lunch until you taste these: slippery soba tossed in a nutty sesame-ginger dressing, with crisp ribbons of carrot, cabbage and cucumber and bright green edamame. It's cool, crunchy and deeply satisfying on a warm day, and it packs beautifully in a lunchbox.

### Ingredients

* 4 oz (115 g) soba (buckwheat) noodles or whole-wheat noodles
* 1 1/2 cups (225 g) frozen shelled edamame
* 1 carrot, cut into matchsticks or grated
* 1 small red bell pepper, thinly sliced
* 1 cup (70 g) red cabbage, finely shredded
* 1/4 cucumber, cut into matchsticks
* 2 scallions, thinly sliced
* 2 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro
* 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

#### For the dressing

* 2 tbsp tahini or natural peanut butter
* 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce or tamari
* 1 tbsp rice vinegar
* 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
* 1 tsp maple syrup
* 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
* 1 small garlic clove, grated
* 2 to 3 tbsp warm water

### Method

1. Cook the noodles according to the package instructions, adding the edamame for the last 3 minutes. Drain, rinse under cold water and drain well.
2. Whisk all the dressing ingredients together, adding enough warm water to make a smooth, pourable dressing.
3. In a large bowl, toss the noodles and edamame with the carrot, pepper, cabbage, cucumber and most of the scallions.
4. Pour over the dressing and toss until everything is coated.
5. Divide between bowls or containers and top with the remaining scallions, cilantro and sesame seeds.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use 100% buckwheat soba or rice noodles, and tamari instead of soy sauce.
- **More protein:** add cubes of baked tofu, shredded chicken or cooked shrimp.
- **Lower carb:** replace half the noodles with spiralized zucchini or extra cabbage.
- **Storage:** soba can stick together after a day or two; loosen with a splash of water or extra dressing.
:::

:::why
Edamame are young soybeans, rich in protein, fiber and isoflavones. Tahini adds calcium and healthy fats, and the raw vegetables provide fiber, vitamin C and crunch.
:::

@recipe Black Bean, Corn & Avocado Tacos
@tags GF | DF | VG | 30 min
@meta Serves 3 | Prep 15 min | Cook 10 min | Best fresh | Protein ≈20 g
Inspired by Mexican street food, these are tacos for when lunch needs to feel like a small celebration. Smoky, cumin-spiced black beans, sweet corn, a sharp lime slaw and creamy avocado, all piled into warm corn tortillas. They're ready in the time it takes to warm the tortillas, and they're naturally gluten-free and vegan.

### Ingredients

* 1 tbsp olive oil
* 1/2 red onion, finely chopped
* 1 garlic clove, crushed
* 1 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp smoked paprika
* 2 cans (15 oz / 400 g each) black beans, drained and rinsed
* 1 cup (150 g) corn kernels (frozen, canned or fresh)
* Juice of 1 lime, divided
* 9 small corn tortillas, labeled gluten-free
* 1 ripe avocado, sliced
* 1/4 cup (60 g) salsa or pico de gallo
* Fresh cilantro, to serve

#### For the lime slaw

* 1 1/2 cups (100 g) finely shredded red and green cabbage
* Juice of 1/2 lime
* 1 tsp olive oil
* A pinch of salt

### Method

1. Toss the slaw ingredients together in a bowl and set aside.
2. Heat the olive oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until softened. Add the garlic, cumin and paprika and cook for 30 seconds.
3. Add the black beans and corn with 3 tbsp water. Cook for 4 to 5 minutes, lightly crushing some of the beans with a fork. Add half the lime juice and season to taste.
4. Warm the tortillas in a dry frying pan for 20 to 30 seconds per side, or wrap in a damp paper towel and microwave for 30 seconds.
5. Fill each tortilla with the bean mixture, slaw, avocado and salsa. Finish with cilantro and the remaining lime juice.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** check that your corn tortillas are 100% corn and labeled gluten-free.
- **More protein:** add a spoonful of Greek yogurt (not vegan) or crumbled feta, or serve with a side of scrambled eggs.
- **Make it a bowl:** serve the beans and toppings over brown rice or quinoa for a packed lunch.
- **Mild:** choose a mild salsa if spicy food triggers hot flashes.
:::

:::why
Black beans provide protein, fiber, folate and magnesium. Avocado adds healthy fats and fiber, cabbage brings vitamin C, and corn tortillas are a naturally whole-grain base.
:::

@recipe Tuscan White Bean & Kale Soup
@tags GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 35 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈20 g
Rustic, cheap and deeply comforting, this is the soup for a gray Sunday so the week has a head start. Mashing some of the beans in the pot gives it a creamy body without any cream, while rosemary, garlic and a squeeze of lemon keep it bright. It freezes beautifully, so make a big pot and stash portions for the days you can't face cooking.

### Ingredients

* 2 tbsp olive oil
* 1 onion, finely chopped
* 2 carrots, diced
* 2 celery stalks, diced
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed
* 1 tsp dried rosemary or 1 sprig fresh
* 1/2 tsp dried thyme
* 1/4 tsp chili flakes (optional)
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 g) chopped tomatoes
* 5 cups (1.2 liters) low-sodium gluten-free vegetable stock
* 3 cans (15 oz / 400 g each) cannellini or other white beans, drained and rinsed
* 1 can (15 oz / 400 g) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 4 cups (150 g) chopped kale, tough stems removed
* Juice of 1/2 lemon
* Salt and black pepper
* Grated Parmesan, to serve (optional; omit for DF and VG, and choose a vegetarian hard cheese if you need it vegetarian)

### Method

1. Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots and celery and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened.
2. Add the garlic, rosemary, thyme and chili flakes (if using) and cook for 1 minute.
3. Add the tomatoes and stock and bring to a boil.
4. Add the beans and chickpeas. Use a potato masher or fork to lightly mash about a quarter of them in the pot, which helps thicken the soup. Reduce the heat and simmer for 15 minutes.
5. Stir in the kale and simmer for 5 to 7 minutes more, until tender.
6. Add the lemon juice and season to taste. Serve topped with a little Parmesan if you like, or leave it off to keep the soup dairy-free and vegan (traditional Parmesan isn't vegetarian either).

:::swaps
- **More protein:** stir in cooked shredded chicken, or serve with a slice of seeded rye toast topped with hummus.
- **Greens:** swap the kale for spinach, Swiss chard or cavolo nero.
- **Gluten-free:** check that your stock is gluten-free.
- **Freezing:** cool quickly and freeze in portions for up to 3 months. Defrost overnight in the fridge and reheat until steaming hot.
:::

:::why
White beans are an excellent source of plant protein, fiber and potassium. Kale provides calcium, vitamin K and folate, and the soup's high water content helps hydration.
:::

@recipe Tuna & White Bean Niçoise Salad
@tags GF | DF | 30 min
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 15 min | Cook 12 min | Keeps 2 days | Protein ≈30 g
The classic French salad, reworked for a hungry midweek lunch. Creamy white beans join the new potatoes for extra fiber, a jammy egg and good canned tuna do the anchoring, and a sharp mustard vinaigrette pulls it all together. It looks elegant enough for guests and costs very little to make.

### Ingredients

* 8 oz (225 g) small new potatoes, halved
* 1 cup (100 g) green beans, trimmed
* 2 eggs
* 1 can (5 oz / 140 g) tuna in olive oil or water, drained
* 1 can (15 oz / 400 g) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
* 1 cup (150 g) cherry tomatoes, halved
* 1 head little gem or romaine lettuce, leaves separated
* 1/4 cup (35 g) black olives
* 1 tbsp capers (optional)

#### For the dressing

* 3 tbsp olive oil
* 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
* 1 tsp Dijon mustard
* 1 tsp chopped fresh chives or parsley
* Salt and black pepper

### Method

1. Put the potatoes in a pan of cold water, bring to a boil and cook for 10 to 12 minutes until tender. Add the green beans for the last 3 minutes. Drain and cool under cold water.
2. Meanwhile, bring a small pan of water to a boil. Gently lower in the eggs and cook for 7 to 8 minutes. Cool in cold water, then peel and halve.
3. Whisk the dressing ingredients together.
4. Arrange the lettuce on plates or in containers. Top with the potatoes, green beans, white beans, tomatoes, tuna, olives and capers.
5. Add the eggs and drizzle with the dressing just before serving.

:::swaps
- **Fish swap:** use canned salmon, sardines or leftover grilled salmon for extra omega-3s.
- **Vegetarian:** replace the tuna with an extra egg and chickpeas.
- **Packing:** keep the dressing separate and add just before eating.
- **Mercury note:** canned light tuna (skipjack) is lower in mercury than albacore; mixing up your fish choices through the week is a good idea.
:::

:::why
Tuna and eggs provide high-quality protein and vitamin B12, white beans add fiber, and the vegetables provide vitamin C and folate. Olive oil and olives contribute healthy fats.
:::

@recipe Quinoa Tabbouleh with Chickpeas
@tags GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 3 | Prep 20 min | Cook 15 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈20 g
Real tabbouleh is more herb than grain, and this version keeps that spirit: armfuls of parsley and mint, juicy tomatoes and cucumber, and plenty of lemon. Quinoa stands in for bulgur, so it's naturally gluten-free, and chickpeas make it a complete lunch. It's the kind of salad that makes you feel brighter just looking at it.

### Ingredients

* 3/4 cup (130 g) quinoa, rinsed
* 1 1/2 cups (360 ml) water
* 2 cans (15 oz / 400 g each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 1 large bunch (60 g) fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
* 1 small bunch (15 g) fresh mint, finely chopped
* 1 1/2 cups (225 g) cherry tomatoes, quartered
* 1/2 cucumber, finely diced
* 3 scallions, thinly sliced
* 1/4 cup (60 ml) olive oil
* Juice of 1 1/2 lemons
* 1/2 tsp ground cumin
* Salt and black pepper
* 2 tbsp pomegranate seeds or toasted pine nuts, to serve (optional)

### Method

1. Put the quinoa and water in a saucepan with a pinch of salt. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently for 12 to 15 minutes, until the water is absorbed. Remove from the heat, leave covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork and spread on a plate to cool.
2. In a large bowl, combine the cooled quinoa, chickpeas, parsley, mint, tomatoes, cucumber and scallions.
3. Whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice, cumin, salt and pepper. Pour over the salad and toss well.
4. Taste and add more lemon or salt if needed. Top with pomegranate seeds or pine nuts if you like.

:::swaps
- **More protein:** add crumbled feta (not vegan), grilled chicken, baked falafel or a side of hummus.
- **Grain swap:** if you don't need it gluten-free, use bulgur wheat for a traditional tabbouleh.
- **Make it a wrap:** stuff into a whole-grain pita with hummus.
:::

:::why
Quinoa and chickpeas together provide plant protein and fiber. Parsley is rich in vitamin C, vitamin K and folate, and the olive oil and lemon dressing adds healthy fats and fresh flavor.
:::

@recipe Miso-Glazed Tofu Buddha Bowl
@tags DF | VG | GF option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 15 min | Cook 25 min | Keeps 3 days | Protein ≈40 g
Golden, sticky cubes of tofu with a sweet-salty miso glaze, over nutty brown rice with crisp vegetables and a creamy ginger dressing. It's the bowl to reach for when you want something that feels nourishing and a little indulgent at the same time. Miso, a fermented soybean paste, brings deep savory flavor and live cultures.

### Ingredients

* 1 block (14 oz / 400 g) extra-firm tofu, pressed and cut into cubes
* 1 tbsp white miso paste
* 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce or tamari
* 1 tsp maple syrup
* 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
* 1 cup (190 g) cooked brown rice per person (or quinoa)
* 1 cup (90 g) broccoli florets
* 1 carrot, grated or ribboned
* 1/2 cup (75 g) shelled edamame, thawed
* 1/2 avocado, sliced
* 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds

#### For the dressing

* 1 tbsp white miso paste
* 1 tbsp tahini
* 1 tbsp rice vinegar
* 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
* 2 tbsp warm water

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a baking tray with parchment.
2. Whisk together the miso, soy sauce, maple syrup and sesame oil. Toss the tofu in the mixture until coated.
3. Spread the tofu on the tray and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, turning halfway, until golden and slightly crisp.
4. Meanwhile, steam or microwave the broccoli for 3 to 4 minutes until just tender.
5. Whisk the dressing ingredients together until smooth.
6. Divide the rice between bowls and top with the tofu, broccoli, carrot, edamame and avocado. Drizzle with the dressing and sprinkle with sesame seeds.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use tamari and a miso labeled gluten-free (some miso contains barley).
- **Quicker:** pan-fry the tofu in a nonstick pan for 8 to 10 minutes, adding the glaze for the last minute.
- **Swap the protein:** use salmon fillets brushed with the same glaze (bake for 12 to 15 minutes).
- **Miso tip:** add miso to the dressing without heating, to keep more of its live cultures.
:::

:::why
Tofu, edamame and miso provide three different forms of soy, with protein and isoflavones. Brown rice adds fiber and B vitamins, and broccoli brings calcium, vitamin C and fiber.
:::

@recipe Lemon Herb Chicken Grain Bowl
@tags DF | GF option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 25 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈40 g
The meal-prep hero of this book. One batch of garlicky, lemon-marinated chicken and a pot of grains gives you four lunches that taste like they came from a Mediterranean café. Any leftover chicken goes straight into wraps and soups later in the week. If you only try one make-ahead lunch, make it this one.

### Ingredients

* 1 1/4 lb (570 g) boneless, skinless chicken breasts or thighs
* 2 tbsp olive oil
* Finely grated zest and juice of 1 lemon
* 2 garlic cloves, crushed
* 1 tsp dried oregano
* 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
* Salt and black pepper
* 3 cups (about 500 g) cooked farro, freekeh or quinoa
* 2 cups (60 g) baby spinach or mixed leaves
* 1 cup (150 g) cherry tomatoes, halved
* 1/2 cucumber, diced
* 1/2 cup (120 g) hummus
* 1/4 cup (35 g) pitted olives (optional)
* Fresh parsley, to serve

### Method

1. In a bowl, mix the olive oil, lemon zest and juice, garlic, oregano, paprika, salt and pepper. Add the chicken and turn to coat. Marinate for at least 15 minutes, or overnight in the fridge.
2. Heat a grill pan or frying pan over medium-high heat. Cook the chicken for 5 to 7 minutes per side (breasts) or 6 to 8 minutes per side (thighs), until cooked through, with no pink meat, and the juices run clear. A meat thermometer should read 165°F (74°C).
3. Rest for 5 minutes, then slice.
4. Divide the grains between four containers or bowls. Top with spinach, tomatoes, cucumber, chicken, a spoonful of hummus and olives. Finish with parsley and an extra squeeze of lemon.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use quinoa or brown rice instead of farro or freekeh.
- **Vegetarian:** replace the chicken with chickpeas roasted in the same marinade at 400°F (200°C) for 25 minutes, or halloumi slices (not dairy-free).
- **Cook once, eat three times:** cook extra chicken to tuck into a whole-grain wrap with hummus and salad, or stir into Tuscan White Bean & Kale Soup.
:::

:::why
Chicken provides lean, high-quality protein to protect muscle. Whole grains add fiber and B vitamins, hummus contributes plant protein and healthy fats, and the vegetables supply vitamins and antioxidants.
:::

@recipe Spiced Red Lentil Dal with Spinach
@tags GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 10 min | Cook 30 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈20 g
Dal is the dish so many South Asian families turn to at the end of a long day, and once you've made it you'll understand why. Red lentils melt into a thick, golden, gently spiced stew in about 25 minutes, no soaking needed, and a sizzle of popped mustard and cumin seeds on top makes it sing. It's one of the cheapest, most nourishing meals you can cook.

### Ingredients

* 1 tbsp olive oil
* 1 onion, finely chopped
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed
* A thumb-sized piece (1 in / 2.5 cm) of fresh ginger, grated
* 1 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp ground coriander
* 1 tsp ground turmeric
* 1/2 tsp garam masala
* 1 1/2 cups (300 g) red lentils, rinsed well
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 g) chopped tomatoes
* 4 1/2 cups (1.1 liters) low-sodium gluten-free vegetable stock or water
* 3 large handfuls (90 g) baby spinach
* Juice of 1/2 lemon
* Salt, to taste
* Fresh cilantro and plain unsweetened soy or coconut yogurt, to serve
* Cooked brown rice, to serve (or whole-grain flatbread if you don't need it gluten-free)

#### Optional tempering (tadka)

* 1 tsp olive oil
* 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
* 1/2 tsp mustard seeds

### Method

1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 6 to 8 minutes until soft and golden.
2. Add the garlic and ginger and cook for 1 minute. Stir in the cumin, coriander, turmeric and garam masala and cook for 30 seconds, until fragrant.
3. Add the lentils, tomatoes and stock. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer, partly covered, for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are completely soft and the dal is thick and creamy. Add a splash more water if it becomes too thick.
4. Stir in the spinach until wilted. Add the lemon juice and season with salt.
5. For the tadka (optional): heat the oil in a small pan, add the seeds and cook for 30 to 60 seconds until they pop. Pour over the dal.
6. Serve with cilantro, a spoonful of yogurt and brown rice or flatbread.

:::swaps
- **Not vegan?** Cook with ghee instead of oil and serve with dairy yogurt.
- **More protein:** stir in cubes of paneer (not vegan) or tofu, or serve with a boiled egg.
- **Creamier:** replace 1 cup of the stock with light coconut milk.
- **Cook once, eat three times:** serve with rice for dinner, pack for lunch, then thin with stock for a quick soup.
- **Freezing:** freezes well for up to 3 months.
:::

:::why
Red lentils provide plant protein, fiber, iron and folate. Spinach adds more iron and magnesium, and the tomatoes and lemon provide vitamin C, which helps your body absorb the iron. Turmeric, ginger and cumin add flavor and anti-inflammatory compounds.
:::

@recipe Smashed Chickpea Salad Sandwich
@tags DF | VG | GF option | 15 min | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 15 min | Cook None | Keeps 3 days | Protein ≈20 g
Think of everything you love about a deli egg-salad sandwich, creamy, tangy and crunchy, then make it from a can of chickpeas in ten minutes. Celery and red onion bring the crunch, capers and lemon the zing. It's the lunch to turn to when the fridge is nearly empty and you still want something to look forward to.

### Ingredients

* 1 can (15 oz / 400 g) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 2 tbsp tahini or vegan mayonnaise
* 1 tsp Dijon mustard
* Juice of 1/2 lemon
* 1 celery stalk, finely diced
* 1/4 red onion, finely diced
* 1 tbsp capers or chopped pickles
* 1 tbsp chopped fresh dill or parsley
* Salt and black pepper
* 4 slices whole-grain or seeded bread
* 1 large handful lettuce or arugula
* 1 tomato, sliced

### Method

1. Put the chickpeas in a bowl and mash with a fork or potato masher until mostly broken down, leaving some texture.
2. Add the tahini, mustard and lemon juice and mix well.
3. Stir in the celery, red onion, capers and herbs. Season with salt and pepper.
4. Spread the filling over two slices of bread, top with lettuce and tomato, and close the sandwiches.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use gluten-free bread or serve in lettuce cups or with rice cakes.
- **Not vegan:** use Greek yogurt or regular mayonnaise instead of tahini.
- **Flavor twists:** add curry powder and raisins, or chopped sun-dried tomatoes and basil.
- **Packing:** pack the filling separately and assemble at lunchtime so the bread doesn't go soggy.
:::

:::why
Chickpeas provide plant protein, fiber and small amounts of isoflavones. Tahini adds calcium and healthy fats, and whole-grain bread contributes extra fiber and B vitamins.
:::
