@front How to Use This Book
This book is meant to be used, not just read. Dip in wherever you need to, on whatever kind of day you're having. There's no single right way through it, but there are three easy ways in.

## Three Ways In

**Read it cover to cover.** If you like to understand the "why" before the "how," start with Part One, which walks through each stage of the transition in plain language. Part Two covers treatments, therapies and the everyday habits with the strongest evidence behind them. Part Three turns all of it into food.

**Go straight to what's bothering you.** If hot flashes, broken sleep or afternoon crashes are your biggest problem right now, turn to Chapter 10, "Eating for Your Symptoms." Each symptom has its own section, with what may help, what may make it worse and one or two things to try this week. There's also a symptom index at the back.

**Start cooking tonight.** Read Chapter 8, "Not Sure of Your Stage? Start Here," and Chapter 12, "The Busy Woman's Kitchen," then turn to the 12-week meal plan in Part Five. You can come back to the background chapters whenever you're curious.

## The One Idea to Remember: Anchor, Add, Adapt

If you only take one thing from this book, take these three words. They're the method behind every chapter, recipe and meal plan in it.

- **Anchor** every meal with protein, about 25 to 30 grams. It's the nutrient midlife muscles need most and the one most of us eat least of in the morning.
- **Add** plants: vegetables, fruit, beans, whole grains, nuts and seeds, aiming for 30 different ones a week.
- **Adapt** to where you are: your stage of the transition, your symptoms, your appetite and your life this week.

You'll meet Anchor, Add, Adapt properly in Chapter 9, and you'll see it at work in every recipe.

## Take It One Change at a Time

You don't need to overhaul your life this week. In fact, it works better if you don't. Pick one change that feels doable, like adding protein to breakfast or drinking a glass of water before your morning coffee, and let it settle into routine before you add the next.

:::note Keep your doctor in the loop
This book offers general, evidence-informed guidance, but it can't know your personal health history. If you have a medical condition, take regular medication, are thinking about hormone therapy or supplements, or have symptoms that worry you, talk to your doctor or another qualified healthcare professional. A good clinician is one of the best allies you can have during this transition.
:::
