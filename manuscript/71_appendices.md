## Appendix E: Questions to Ask Your Healthcare Provider

**Before your appointment**

[ ] Bring 4 to 8 weeks of your Symptom & Mood Tracker (Appendix A).
[ ] List your medicines and supplements, with doses.
[ ] Write down your top two concerns, so they come first.
[ ] Note the date of your last period and any changes to your cycle.
[ ] Consider bringing a partner or friend to help you remember what was said.

**Questions to ask**

[ ] Are my symptoms likely to be caused by perimenopause or menopause?
[ ] Do I need any tests, and why?
[ ] Is hormone therapy a suitable option for me? What are the benefits and risks in my situation?
[ ] Which type and form would you recommend, and why?
[ ] What non-hormonal options could help, and what monitoring would they need?
[ ] Could vaginal estrogen help with dryness, discomfort or bladder symptoms?
[ ] Could my current medicines or supplements affect my symptoms or interact with treatment?
[ ] Do I need a bone density scan, or cholesterol, blood sugar, thyroid, iron, vitamin D or B12 tests?
[ ] Do I still need contraception, and until when?
[ ] Can you refer me for CBT, pelvic floor therapy, a dietitian or a menopause specialist?
[ ] When should we review how things are going?

## Appendix F: Trusted Resources & Organizations

These organizations offer reliable, evidence-based information. Website addresses change, so search for the organization's name if a link doesn't work.

- **The Menopause Society** (menopause.org): information for women and a directory of certified menopause practitioners.
- **Office on Women's Health, US Department of Health and Human Services** (womenshealth.gov): plain-language information on menopause and women's health.
- **National Institute on Aging** (nia.nih.gov): information on menopause, healthy aging, exercise and memory.
- **NIH Office of Dietary Supplements** (ods.od.nih.gov): fact sheets on vitamins, minerals and supplements.
- **Dietary Guidelines for Americans** (dietaryguidelines.gov) and **MyPlate** (myplate.gov).
- **Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics** (eatright.org): find a registered dietitian near you.
- **Bone Health & Osteoporosis Foundation** (bonehealthandosteoporosis.org): bone health, exercise and fracture prevention.
- **American Heart Association** (heart.org): heart health for women.
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline**: call or text 988 in the United States, any time.
- **FORCE** (facingourrisk.org): support for people with inherited cancer risk, including BRCA mutations.
- **The Daisy Network** (daisynetwork.org): support for women with premature (primary) ovarian insufficiency.
- **NHS** (nhs.uk) and **NICE** (nice.org.uk): UK menopause information and guidance.
