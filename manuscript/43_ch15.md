Dinner is the meal most of us already build around protein. The challenge is making it easy enough for a Tuesday, when you're tired and the day has used you up. Every recipe in this chapter delivers at least 25 grams of protein per serving, and most come together in a single pan, pot or slow cooker. Many make enough for lunch the next day, or for the freezer, so tonight's cooking also covers a busier night later in the week.

If you're cooking for a family, these recipes scale easily, and most can be adapted at the table: chili and spice on the side, extra grains for the hungriest eaters, and plenty of vegetables for everyone.

@recipe Sheet-Pan Salmon with Broccoli & Sweet Potato
@tags GF | DF | 30 min
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 10 min | Cook 20 min | Keeps 2 days | Protein ≈30 g
This is the dinner that converts people to sheet-pan cooking. The sweet potatoes go in first and turn golden at the edges, then the salmon and broccoli join them for the last few minutes, all on one tray, with one pan to wash. A sticky glaze of maple, mustard and lemon makes it taste far more effortful than it is.

### Ingredients

* 2 medium sweet potatoes (about 1 lb / 450 g), cut into 3/4-inch (2 cm) cubes
* 2 tbsp olive oil, divided
* 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
* Salt and black pepper
* 1 large head broccoli (about 12 oz / 340 g), cut into florets
* 4 salmon fillets (about 5 oz / 140 g each), skin on or off
* 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
* 1 tbsp maple syrup
* Finely grated zest and juice of 1 lemon
* 1 garlic clove, grated
* 1 tbsp sesame seeds or chopped fresh dill, to serve

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) and line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment.
2. Toss the sweet potatoes with 1 tbsp of the oil, the paprika and a pinch of salt and pepper. Spread them on the sheet and roast for 10 minutes.
3. Meanwhile, toss the broccoli with the remaining oil and a pinch of salt. Whisk the mustard, maple syrup, lemon zest, half the lemon juice and the garlic together.
4. Push the sweet potatoes to the sides, add the broccoli, and place the salmon in the center. Brush the salmon generously with the glaze.
5. Roast for 10 to 12 minutes more, until the salmon flakes easily and reaches 145°F (63°C), and the broccoli is tender with crisp edges.
6. Squeeze over the remaining lemon juice and scatter with sesame seeds or dill.

:::swaps
- **Fish swap:** use trout, arctic char or cod (cod may need 2 to 3 minutes less).
- **Vegetarian:** use thick slices of extra-firm tofu brushed with the same glaze.
- **Cook once, eat three times:** flake leftover salmon into a grain bowl with greens for lunch, or mash it with Greek yogurt and herbs to spread on rye crackers.
:::

:::why
Salmon is one of the richest sources of omega-3 fats and also provides vitamin D. Broccoli adds fiber, vitamin C and some calcium, and sweet potatoes bring slow-release carbohydrate and beta-carotene.
:::

@recipe Chicken Pozole Verde
@tags GF | DF | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 6 | Prep 20 min | Cook 45 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈30 g
Pozole is a celebratory Mexican soup-stew built on hominy, big chewy corn kernels with a flavor all their own. This green version gets its bright, tangy body from tomatillos, poblano peppers and cilantro, and it's the kind of pot that fills the house with good smells. The real joy is the toppings: crunchy radishes, shredded cabbage, avocado and a squeeze of lime, piled on at the table.

### Ingredients

* 1 lb (450 g) tomatillos, husked and rinsed (or a 28 oz / 800 g can, drained)
* 2 poblano peppers, halved and seeded
* 1 jalapeño, halved and seeded (optional)
* 1 onion, quartered
* 4 garlic cloves, peeled
* 1 large bunch (60 g) fresh cilantro, stems and leaves
* 2 lb (900 g) boneless, skinless chicken thighs
* 6 cups (1.4 liters) low-sodium chicken stock
* 2 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp dried oregano (Mexican if you can find it)
* 2 cans (15 oz / 425 g each) white hominy, drained and rinsed
* Salt, to taste

#### To serve

* Shredded green cabbage, sliced radishes, diced avocado, lime wedges and extra cilantro

### Method

1. Heat the broiler. Spread the tomatillos, poblanos, jalapeño (if using), onion and garlic on a baking sheet and broil for 8 to 10 minutes, turning once, until charred in places and softened.
2. Transfer everything to a blender with the cilantro and 1 cup (240 ml) of the stock. Blend until smooth.
3. Put the chicken, remaining stock, cumin and oregano in a large pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer for 20 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through.
4. Lift out the chicken and shred it with two forks. Return it to the pot with the green sauce and hominy.
5. Simmer for 15 minutes more, until slightly thickened. Season with salt.
6. Serve in deep bowls, with the toppings in small dishes so everyone can add their own.

:::swaps
- **Slow cooker:** after step 2, put everything except the hominy in a slow cooker and cook on low for 6 hours. Shred the chicken, add the hominy and cook for 30 minutes more.
- **Vegetarian:** replace the chicken with 2 cans (15 oz / 425 g each) white beans and use vegetable stock (protein will be lower, about 15 g a serving).
- **Mild:** leave out the jalapeño and keep the heat at the table.
- **Freezing:** freeze without the toppings for up to 3 months.
:::

:::why
Chicken thighs provide generous protein, and hominy adds fiber and slow-release carbohydrate. The crunchy toppings bring vitamin C, fiber and healthy fats, and the tomatillo sauce delivers a lot of flavor for very little salt or fat.
:::

@recipe Three-Bean Vegetable Chili
@tags GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 6 | Prep 15 min | Cook 40 min | Keeps 5 days | Protein ≈25 g
A big, smoky pot of chili is the ultimate cook-once meal. Three kinds of beans and crumbled tofu make it hearty enough to satisfy the most committed meat-eater, and a spoonful of cocoa powder and a little cinnamon give it a deep, rounded flavor. It tastes even better on the second day.

### Ingredients

* 2 tbsp olive oil
* 1 large onion, diced
* 1 red and 1 green bell pepper, diced
* 2 carrots, diced
* 4 garlic cloves, crushed
* 1 block (16 oz / 450 g) extra-firm tofu, pressed and crumbled
* 2 tbsp chili powder (mild or medium)
* 2 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp smoked paprika
* 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
* 1 tbsp unsweetened cocoa powder
* 2 tbsp tomato paste
* 1 can (28 oz / 800 g) crushed tomatoes
* 4 cans (15 oz / 425 g each) mixed beans, such as kidney, black and pinto, drained and rinsed
* 2 cups (480 ml) low-sodium vegetable stock
* 1 cup (150 g) frozen corn
* Juice of 1 lime
* Salt and black pepper

#### To serve

* Avocado, sliced scallions, cilantro, and plain soy yogurt (or Greek yogurt if not vegan)

### Method

1. Heat the oil in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion, peppers and carrots and cook for 8 minutes, until softened.
2. Add the garlic and crumbled tofu and cook for 5 minutes, stirring, until the tofu starts to brown.
3. Stir in the chili powder, cumin, paprika, cinnamon, cocoa and tomato paste and cook for 1 minute.
4. Add the tomatoes, beans and stock. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer, partly covered, for 25 minutes, stirring occasionally.
5. Stir in the corn and cook for 5 minutes. Add the lime juice and season to taste.
6. Serve with your favorite toppings.

:::swaps
- **Not vegetarian:** replace the tofu with 1 lb (450 g) lean ground turkey, browned in step 2.
- **Slow cooker:** cook steps 1 to 3 in a pan, then transfer everything except the corn and lime to a slow cooker and cook on low for 6 to 8 hours.
- **Cook once, eat three times:** spoon over a baked sweet potato for lunch, or wrap in a whole-grain tortilla with avocado.
- **Freezing:** freeze in portions for up to 3 months.
:::

:::why
Beans and tofu together deliver plant protein, plenty of fiber, iron and magnesium. Tofu adds soy isoflavones, and the tomatoes and peppers bring vitamin C, which helps your body absorb the iron from the beans.
:::

@recipe Baked Cod with Tomatoes, Olives & Capers
@tags GF | DF | 30 min
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 10 min | Cook 18 min | Keeps 2 days | Protein ≈35 g
A taste of the Mediterranean in under half an hour. Cod bakes gently in a bed of cherry tomatoes, olives, capers and white beans, which burst and melt into a silky, salty-sweet sauce. Serve with crusty bread to mop up the juices, or a pile of greens.

### Ingredients

* 2 tbsp olive oil
* 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
* 2 cups (300 g) cherry tomatoes, halved
* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
* 1/3 cup (50 g) pitted Kalamata olives, halved
* 2 tbsp capers, drained
* 1/2 tsp dried oregano
* A pinch of chili flakes (optional)
* 4 cod fillets (about 6 oz / 170 g each)
* Salt and black pepper
* Finely grated zest of 1 lemon
* A handful of fresh parsley or basil, chopped
* Lemon wedges, to serve

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C).
2. In a large ovenproof dish, toss the olive oil, garlic, tomatoes, beans, olives, capers, oregano and chili flakes (if using). Bake for 8 minutes, until the tomatoes start to soften.
3. Season the cod lightly with salt and pepper. Nestle the fillets into the tomato mixture and spoon some of the juices over them.
4. Bake for 10 to 12 minutes, until the fish is opaque and flakes easily, reaching 145°F (63°C).
5. Scatter with lemon zest and herbs and serve with lemon wedges.

:::swaps
- **Fish swap:** use halibut, haddock, pollock or salmon (salmon may need a few minutes longer).
- **Vegetarian:** skip the fish and add a second can of beans and some crumbled feta.
- **Low-sodium:** rinse the capers and olives well, and add no extra salt.
:::

:::why
Cod is a lean, mild source of high-quality protein. White beans add fiber and more protein, tomatoes bring vitamin C and lycopene, and olive oil and olives provide heart-healthy fats.
:::

@recipe Ginger-Garlic Tofu & Vegetable Stir-Fry
@tags DF | VG | GF option | 30 min
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 15 min | Keeps 3 days | Protein ≈35 g
Faster than takeout and full of crunch. Crispy cubes of tofu and edamame tumble through a wok full of bright vegetables, then get coated in a glossy ginger-garlic sauce. Serve it over brown rice or noodles and you'll wonder why you ever waited for delivery.

### Ingredients

* 2 blocks (14 oz / 400 g each) extra-firm tofu, pressed and cut into cubes
* 1 tbsp cornstarch
* 2 tbsp neutral oil, such as avocado or canola, divided
* 1 red bell pepper, sliced
* 2 cups (180 g) broccoli florets
* 1 cup (100 g) snow peas or snap peas
* 1 carrot, thinly sliced on the diagonal
* 1 cup (150 g) frozen shelled edamame, thawed
* 3 scallions, sliced
* Cooked brown rice or noodles, to serve

#### For the sauce

* 3 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce or tamari
* 1 tbsp rice vinegar
* 1 tbsp maple syrup
* 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
* 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
* 3 garlic cloves, grated
* 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1/4 cup (60 ml) water

### Method

1. Whisk the sauce ingredients together.
2. Toss the tofu with the cornstarch. Heat 1 tbsp of the oil in a large wok or nonstick frying pan over medium-high heat. Cook the tofu for 8 to 10 minutes, turning, until golden and crisp. Transfer to a plate.
3. Add the remaining oil, then the pepper, broccoli, snow peas and carrot. Stir-fry for 4 to 5 minutes, until bright and just tender. Add a splash of water if the pan gets dry.
4. Return the tofu to the pan with the edamame. Pour in the sauce and toss for 1 to 2 minutes, until glossy and thickened.
5. Scatter with scallions and serve over brown rice or noodles.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use tamari labeled gluten-free.
- **Not vegetarian:** use 1 1/4 lb (570 g) sliced chicken breast or shrimp instead of tofu.
- **Use what you have:** any quick-cooking vegetables work, including bok choy, mushrooms, zucchini and baby corn.
:::

:::why
Tofu and edamame provide plenty of plant protein and soy isoflavones, and calcium-set tofu boosts calcium too. The vegetables add fiber, vitamin C and folate, and ginger brings flavor along with natural anti-inflammatory compounds.
:::

@recipe Lentil Bolognese with Whole-Grain Pasta
@tags DF | VG | GF option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 40 min | Keeps 5 days | Protein ≈25 g
All the slow-simmered comfort of a classic Bolognese, made with lentils instead of beef. The lentils soften into the rich tomato sauce, a splash of red wine vinegar brightens it, and a handful of chopped walnuts gives it a surprisingly meaty depth. The sauce freezes perfectly, so make a double batch.

### Ingredients

* 2 tbsp olive oil
* 1 onion, finely chopped
* 1 carrot, finely chopped
* 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
* 8 oz (225 g) mushrooms, finely chopped
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed
* 2 tbsp tomato paste
* 1 cup (200 g) dried brown or green lentils, rinsed
* 1 can (28 oz / 800 g) crushed tomatoes
* 2 1/2 cups (600 ml) low-sodium vegetable stock
* 1 tsp dried oregano
* 1 bay leaf
* 1/3 cup (40 g) walnuts, finely chopped
* 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
* Salt and black pepper
* 12 oz (340 g) whole-grain spaghetti or penne
* Fresh basil and nutritional yeast, to serve

### Method

1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot and celery and cook for 8 minutes, until soft.
2. Add the mushrooms and cook for 5 minutes, until they've released their liquid. Stir in the garlic and tomato paste and cook for 1 minute.
3. Add the lentils, tomatoes, stock, oregano and bay leaf. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer, partly covered, for 30 to 35 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are tender and the sauce is thick. Add a splash of water if it gets too thick.
4. Meanwhile, cook the pasta according to the package instructions.
5. Remove the bay leaf. Stir in the walnuts and vinegar, and season.
6. Toss with the pasta, or spoon the sauce over it, and top with basil and a sprinkle of nutritional yeast.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use gluten-free pasta (chickpea or lentil pasta adds even more protein).
- **Not vegan:** finish with grated Parmesan.
- **Freezing:** freeze the sauce in portions for up to 3 months.
:::

:::why
Lentils provide plant protein, fiber, iron and folate, and whole-grain pasta adds more protein and fiber than white pasta. Walnuts contribute plant omega-3s, and the tomatoes bring lycopene and vitamin C.
:::

@recipe Greek Turkey Meatballs with Tzatziki
@tags GF option | DF option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 20 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈35 g
Juicy, herb-flecked meatballs with oregano, lemon and a little feta, baked rather than fried, and served with a cool, garlicky tzatziki. Pile them into whole-grain pitas with tomatoes and cucumber, or serve them over a big Greek salad. They freeze beautifully, so it's worth making a double batch.

### Ingredients

#### For the meatballs (makes about 20)

* 1 1/4 lb (570 g) lean ground turkey (93% lean)
* 1 egg
* 1/3 cup (30 g) rolled oats or breadcrumbs
* 1/3 cup (50 g) crumbled feta cheese
* 2 garlic cloves, grated
* 2 tbsp chopped fresh dill or parsley
* 1 tsp dried oregano
* Finely grated zest of 1 lemon
* 1/2 tsp salt and plenty of black pepper

#### For the tzatziki

* 1 cup (225 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 1/2 cucumber, grated and squeezed dry
* 1 small garlic clove, grated
* 1 tbsp chopped fresh dill or mint
* 1 tbsp lemon juice
* A pinch of salt

#### To serve

* Whole-grain pitas or a large Greek salad (tomatoes, cucumber, red onion, olives, romaine)

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment.
2. In a large bowl, mix all the meatball ingredients gently with your hands until just combined. Don't overwork the mixture, or the meatballs will be tough.
3. Roll into about 20 balls the size of a walnut and place on the baking sheet.
4. Bake for 18 to 20 minutes, until golden and cooked through (165°F / 74°C in the center).
5. Meanwhile, stir together the tzatziki ingredients.
6. Serve the meatballs with tzatziki, in pitas or over salad.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use certified gluten-free oats and serve over salad or rice instead of pita.
- **Dairy-free:** leave out the feta and make the tzatziki with unsweetened soy yogurt.
- **Freezing:** freeze cooked meatballs for up to 3 months; reheat from frozen at 350°F (180°C) for 15 to 20 minutes.
:::

:::why
Lean turkey is an excellent source of protein, and the tzatziki adds more protein and calcium from Greek yogurt. Serving them with salad or whole-grain pita adds fiber, and herbs and lemon bring lots of flavor with little salt.
:::

@recipe Coconut Chickpea & Vegetable Curry
@tags GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 30 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈25 g
A creamy, golden curry that's gentle enough for the whole family yet full of flavor. Chickpeas and golden cubes of tofu simmer with cauliflower and spinach in a light coconut sauce fragrant with ginger, garlic and garam masala. It's even better reheated the next day.

### Ingredients

* 1 block (14 oz / 400 g) firm tofu, pressed and cut into cubes
* 2 tbsp neutral oil, divided
* 1 onion, finely chopped
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed
* A thumb-sized piece (1 in / 2.5 cm) of fresh ginger, grated
* 2 tbsp mild curry powder
* 1 tsp garam masala
* 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 g) chopped tomatoes
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 ml) light coconut milk
* 1/2 cup (120 ml) low-sodium vegetable stock
* 2 cans (15 oz / 425 g each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 1/2 small cauliflower, cut into small florets
* 3 large handfuls (90 g) baby spinach
* Juice of 1/2 lime
* Salt, to taste
* Cilantro and cooked brown rice, to serve

### Method

1. Heat 1 tbsp of the oil in a large deep pan over medium-high heat. Cook the tofu for 6 to 8 minutes, turning, until golden. Set aside.
2. Reduce the heat to medium and add the remaining oil and the onion. Cook for 6 minutes, until soft. Add the garlic and ginger and cook for 1 minute.
3. Stir in the curry powder, garam masala and turmeric and cook for 30 seconds.
4. Add the tomatoes, coconut milk, stock, chickpeas and cauliflower. Simmer for 15 minutes, until the cauliflower is tender.
5. Return the tofu, stir in the spinach until wilted, and add the lime juice and salt to taste.
6. Serve over brown rice, scattered with cilantro.

:::swaps
- **More heat:** add a chopped green chili with the garlic, or serve with chili on the side.
- **Not vegan:** replace the tofu with paneer, or stir in cooked chicken.
- **Vegetables:** swap the cauliflower for sweet potato, green beans or bell peppers.
:::

:::why
Chickpeas and tofu provide plant protein and fiber, and tofu adds soy isoflavones and calcium. Spinach brings folate and magnesium, and turmeric, ginger and garlic add flavor along with natural anti-inflammatory compounds.
:::

@recipe Shrimp & Vegetable Fajita Bowls
@tags GF | DF | 30 min
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 12 min | Keeps 2 days | Protein ≈35 g
All the sizzle of restaurant fajitas, without the tortilla overload. Smoky, lime-bright shrimp and charred peppers sit on a bed of brown rice and black beans, finished with avocado and a spoonful of salsa. It's a build-your-own dinner that the whole family can customize.

### Ingredients

* 1 lb (450 g) large shrimp, peeled and deveined
* 2 tbsp olive oil, divided
* 2 tsp chili powder
* 1 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp smoked paprika
* 1/2 tsp garlic powder
* Juice of 1 lime, plus wedges to serve
* 3 bell peppers in mixed colors, sliced
* 1 red onion, sliced
* 3 cups (about 600 g) cooked brown rice
* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) black beans, drained, rinsed and warmed
* 1 avocado, sliced
* Salsa, cilantro and plain Greek yogurt or soy yogurt, to serve

### Method

1. In a bowl, toss the shrimp with 1 tbsp of the oil, the spices and half the lime juice.
2. Heat the remaining oil in a large skillet over high heat. Cook the peppers and onion for 6 to 7 minutes, stirring now and then, until softened and charred at the edges. Transfer to a plate.
3. Add the shrimp to the hot pan in a single layer and cook for 1 1/2 to 2 minutes per side, until pink and just cooked through.
4. Return the vegetables to the pan with the remaining lime juice and toss.
5. Divide the rice and beans between bowls and top with the shrimp and vegetables, avocado, salsa, cilantro and a spoonful of yogurt.

:::swaps
- **Protein swap:** use sliced chicken breast (cook for 5 to 6 minutes) or extra-firm tofu.
- **Dairy-free:** top with soy yogurt or skip the yogurt.
- **Mild:** reduce the chili powder and serve hot salsa on the side.
:::

:::why
Shrimp is a lean, quick-cooking protein, and black beans add plant protein and fiber. Bell peppers are packed with vitamin C, avocado adds healthy fats, and brown rice brings fiber and B vitamins.
:::

@recipe Quinoa & Black Bean Stuffed Peppers
@tags GF | V | VG option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 40 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈30 g
Bright bell pepper halves, stuffed with a smoky filling of quinoa, black beans, corn and tomatoes, then baked under a blanket of melted cheese. They look impressive, reheat brilliantly for lunch, and come to the table in their own edible bowls.

### Ingredients

* 4 large bell peppers, halved lengthwise and seeded
* 1 tbsp olive oil
* 1 small onion, finely chopped
* 2 garlic cloves, crushed
* 2 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp smoked paprika
* 2 cups (370 g) cooked quinoa (from about 3/4 cup / 130 g dry)
* 2 cans (15 oz / 425 g each) black beans, drained and rinsed
* 1 cup (150 g) corn kernels
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 g) diced tomatoes, drained
* Salt and black pepper
* 1 cup (100 g) shredded cheddar or Monterey Jack cheese
* 1 cup (225 g) plain Greek yogurt, to serve
* Cilantro and lime wedges, to serve

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Arrange the pepper halves cut side up in a large baking dish.
2. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion for 5 minutes, then add the garlic, cumin and paprika and cook for 1 minute.
3. Stir in the quinoa, beans, corn and tomatoes and heat through. Season to taste.
4. Spoon the filling into the peppers, pressing it down gently. Pour 1/2 cup (120 ml) water into the dish around the peppers.
5. Cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes. Uncover, sprinkle with cheese and bake for 10 to 15 minutes more, until the peppers are tender and the cheese is bubbling.
6. Serve with a spoonful of Greek yogurt, cilantro and lime.

:::swaps
- **Vegan:** use a dairy-free cheese (or skip it) and serve with soy yogurt; add 1 cup (150 g) shelled edamame to the filling to keep the protein up.
- **Not vegetarian:** stir 8 oz (225 g) cooked lean ground turkey into the filling.
- **Make ahead:** stuff the peppers up to a day ahead and refrigerate, then bake when needed (add 5 to 10 minutes).
:::

:::why
Quinoa and black beans together provide plant protein and plenty of fiber. Bell peppers are rich in vitamin C, and the cheese and Greek yogurt add calcium and extra protein.
:::

@recipe Slow-Cooker Moroccan Chicken with Apricots
@tags GF | DF | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 6 | Prep 15 min | Cook 6 hr | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈35 g
Ten minutes of chopping in the morning, and dinner is waiting when you walk in the door. Chicken thighs slow-cook with chickpeas, sweet apricots and warm spices like cinnamon, cumin and ginger until they're meltingly tender in a fragrant, gently sweet sauce. Serve with couscous, quinoa or brown rice.

### Ingredients

* 2 1/2 lb (1.1 kg) boneless, skinless chicken thighs
* 1 onion, sliced
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed
* 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
* 2 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp ground coriander
* 1 tsp ground cinnamon
* 1 tsp sweet paprika
* 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 g) chopped tomatoes
* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 1/2 cup (85 g) dried apricots, halved
* 1 cup (240 ml) low-sodium chicken stock
* Finely grated zest of 1 lemon
* Salt and black pepper
* Fresh cilantro and toasted sliced almonds, to serve
* Cooked quinoa, brown rice or whole-wheat couscous, to serve

### Method

1. Put the onion in the slow cooker and arrange the chicken on top.
2. Mix the garlic, ginger and spices with a pinch of salt and pepper, and rub over the chicken.
3. Add the tomatoes, chickpeas, apricots, stock and lemon zest.
4. Cover and cook on low for 6 hours, or on high for 3 to 4 hours, until the chicken is very tender and reaches 165°F (74°C).
5. Lightly shred or break up the chicken with a fork, and season to taste.
6. Serve over quinoa, rice or couscous, scattered with cilantro and almonds.

:::swaps
- **Stovetop:** brown the chicken in a heavy pot, add everything else and simmer, covered, for 40 to 45 minutes.
- **Gluten-free:** serve with quinoa or rice rather than couscous.
- **Vegetarian:** replace the chicken with a second can of chickpeas and 1 lb (450 g) cubed butternut squash; cook on low for 5 hours.
:::

:::why
Chicken thighs deliver plenty of protein, and chickpeas add plant protein and fiber. Dried apricots bring sweetness along with fiber and potassium, and the warming spices add flavor without extra salt.
:::

@recipe West African Peanut & Sweet Potato Stew
@tags GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 35 min | Keeps 5 days | Protein ≈25 g
Inspired by the groundnut stews cooked across West Africa, this is a bowl of pure comfort: sweet potatoes and black-eyed peas simmered in a rich, gently spiced tomato and peanut sauce, with greens stirred in at the end. It's creamy, warming and naturally vegan, and it tastes even better the next day.

### Ingredients

* 1 tbsp neutral oil
* 1 onion, chopped
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed
* A thumb-sized piece (1 in / 2.5 cm) of fresh ginger, grated
* 1 tsp ground cumin
* 1 tsp ground coriander
* 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
* 2 tbsp tomato paste
* 1 can (14 oz / 400 g) chopped tomatoes
* 3 cups (720 ml) low-sodium vegetable stock
* 1 large sweet potato (about 12 oz / 340 g), peeled and cut into cubes
* 1/2 cup (130 g) natural peanut butter (no added sugar)
* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) black-eyed peas, drained and rinsed
* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
* 4 cups (120 g) chopped kale or spinach
* Juice of 1/2 lime
* Salt, to taste
* Chopped roasted peanuts and cilantro, to serve
* Cooked brown rice, to serve

### Method

1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion for 6 minutes, until soft. Add the garlic, ginger, cumin, coriander and cayenne (if using) and cook for 1 minute.
2. Stir in the tomato paste, then the tomatoes, stock and sweet potato. Bring to a boil, reduce the heat and simmer for 15 minutes, until the sweet potato is almost tender.
3. Whisk the peanut butter with a ladleful of the hot liquid until smooth, then stir it into the pot.
4. Add the black-eyed peas and chickpeas and simmer for 10 minutes, stirring often so the stew doesn't catch on the bottom.
5. Stir in the greens and cook for 3 to 5 minutes, until tender. Add the lime juice and season.
6. Serve over brown rice, topped with peanuts and cilantro.

:::swaps
- **Peanut allergy:** use sunflower seed butter and top with toasted pumpkin seeds.
- **Not vegetarian:** add 1 lb (450 g) diced chicken thighs with the sweet potato.
- **Spice:** add a chopped Scotch bonnet or habanero with the onion if you love heat, or keep it mild and serve chili on the side.
- **Freezing:** freeze for up to 3 months.
:::

:::why
Peanuts, black-eyed peas and chickpeas together provide plant protein, fiber and magnesium. Sweet potatoes bring beta-carotene and potassium, and the greens add folate, calcium and vitamin K.
:::
