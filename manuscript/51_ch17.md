> The hardest part of eating well usually isn't the cooking. It's deciding what to cook.

%% "I don't need another diet," Lorraine told the pharmacist, who had suggested the meal plan in a menopause book she'd seen. Lorraine was sixty-two, newly retired from thirty years of night shifts, and still eating like someone who grabbed whatever was in the vending machine at 3 a.m. "It isn't a diet," the pharmacist said. "It's someone else doing the planning for a while." Lorraine bought the book that afternoon. Week one, she followed it to the letter. By week four she was swapping meals around. By week ten she barely needed it at all, which, the pharmacist told her later, was exactly the point.

The 12-week plan in the next chapter puts every recipe in this book to work. It's a ready-made menu for breakfast, lunch and dinner, with snacks, a shopping list and a Sunday prep plan for every week. You can follow it exactly, borrow from it, or simply use it for ideas on the days you're out of them.

## Three Phases, Twelve Themes

The weeks are grouped into three phases. **Foundations** (Weeks 1 to 4) builds the core habits: protein at breakfast, more plants, hydration and comfort on hot days, and bone health. **Build** (Weeks 5 to 8) focuses on the symptoms many women find hardest: blood-sugar swings and cravings, mood, sleep and digestion. **Thrive** (Weeks 9 to 12) brings it all together with plant-powered eating, a batch-cooking week, real-life eating out, and a final week that helps you design your own plan.

Each week has a short focus, so you always know why you're eating what you're eating. Use the end-of-week check-in to notice what's changing.

## How the Numbers Add Up

Every day in the plan is built on Anchor, Add, Adapt. Each breakfast, lunch and dinner provides about 20 to 40 grams of protein, and one or two snacks fill the gap, so each day lands at roughly 80 to 110 grams. That's in line with the 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines range of about 82 to 109 grams a day for a woman weighing around 150 pounds. If you weigh more or less, or your doctor has given you a different target, adjust the snacks or portions to suit you.

The protein figures are estimates, rounded to the nearest 5 grams per recipe. Don't worry about hitting an exact number. The aim is a pattern: protein at every meal, plants on every plate, and a day that keeps you steady.

## Cook Once, Eat Three Times

The plan assumes you're cooking for one or two people and uses leftovers deliberately. Most dinners serve four, so tonight's dinner often becomes tomorrow's lunch, and the big-batch recipes, like the chili, the pozole and the dal, cover several meals across the week or go into the freezer. A Sunday prep session of 60 to 90 minutes, laid out for each week, sets up the breakfasts, lunches and snacks that make the weekdays easy.

If you're cooking for a family, simply scale up the dinners and plan fewer leftover lunches.

## Make It Yours

This plan is a menu to borrow from, never a test. Swap any meal for another with a similar protein figure. Repeat a week you loved. If you don't eat fish, use the vegetarian swaps in the recipes; if you're gluten-free or dairy-free, follow the labels and the Swaps & Variations boxes. Eating out, a busy week or a family celebration doesn't break the plan. Week 11 even builds three meals out into the menu.

And if you miss a few days, don't start again from Week 1. Pick up right where you are, with the next meal.
