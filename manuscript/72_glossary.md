@front Glossary
**Anchor, Add, Adapt:** the method behind this book's meals: anchor every meal with protein, add plants, and adapt to your stage, symptoms and life.

**Bioidentical hormones:** hormones chemically identical to those your body makes. Regulated, approved products exist; custom-compounded versions aren't tested in the same way.

**Bone density (DXA) scan:** a quick, low-dose X-ray that measures bone density and helps assess fracture risk.

**Boxed warning:** the most prominent safety warning the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) can require on a medicine's label.

**CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy):** a structured talking therapy that helps change unhelpful patterns of thinking and behavior; effective for sleep and for how much hot flashes bother you.

**Cortisol:** the body's main stress hormone, which also helps regulate blood sugar, blood pressure and the sleep–wake cycle.

**Early menopause:** menopause between the ages of 40 and 45.

**Estrobolome:** the collection of gut bacteria that help regulate how much estrogen is recycled in the body.

**Estrogen:** the main female sex hormone, made mostly by the ovaries before menopause, with effects on bones, heart, brain, skin and more.

**Fiber:** the part of plant foods your body can't fully digest; it feeds gut bacteria and supports digestion, blood sugar and cholesterol.

**FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone):** a hormone from the brain that tells the ovaries to ripen an egg; it tends to rise during the transition.

**Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM):** dryness, discomfort and bladder symptoms caused by lower estrogen in the vulva, vagina and bladder.

**GLP-1 receptor agonists:** a group of medicines for type 2 diabetes and weight management that act on appetite and blood-sugar hormones.

**Hormone therapy (MHT or HRT):** treatment that replaces estrogen, usually with a progestogen if you have a uterus; the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats.

**Hot flash:** a sudden feeling of heat, often with flushing, sweating and a racing heart, caused by changes in the brain's temperature control.

**Insulin sensitivity:** how well your body responds to insulin, the hormone that moves sugar from your blood into your cells.

**Isoflavones:** plant compounds in soy foods that act like very weak estrogen in the body.

**Lean mass:** everything in the body that isn't fat, including muscle, bone, organs and water.

**Lignans:** plant compounds, found especially in ground flaxseed, with weak estrogen-like effects.

**Menopause:** the point 12 months after your final menstrual period.

**Microbiome:** the community of bacteria and other microbes living in your gut.

**Neurokinin receptor antagonists:** a newer class of non-hormonal medicines for hot flashes that act on temperature-control cells in the brain.

**Night sweats:** hot flashes that happen during sleep.

**Omega-3 fats:** healthy fats found in oily fish, walnuts, flaxseed and chia, important for heart and brain health.

**Osteopenia:** bone density that's lower than normal, but not low enough to be called osteoporosis.

**Osteoporosis:** a condition in which bones become fragile and break more easily.

**Perimenopause:** the years of transition before the final period, when hormones fluctuate and symptoms often begin.

**Phytoestrogens:** plant compounds with a structure similar to estrogen, including isoflavones and lignans.

**Post-menopause:** all the years after menopause.

**Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI):** menopause before the age of 40, also called premature ovarian insufficiency.

**Progesterone and progestogens:** progesterone is a hormone made after ovulation; progestogens are medicines that act like it and protect the uterus during hormone therapy.

**RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance):** the daily amount of a nutrient set to meet the needs of nearly all healthy people. It's a benchmark, not a minimum.

**Sarcopenia:** significant loss of muscle mass and strength with age.

**Surgical menopause:** menopause caused by removal of both ovaries.

**Testosterone:** a hormone made in smaller amounts by women, involved in libido, energy and muscle.

**Vasomotor symptoms:** the medical term for hot flashes and night sweats.

**Visceral fat:** fat stored deep in the abdomen around the organs, linked to heart disease and type 2 diabetes.
