@chapter 16 | 25 Simple Snacks
> A snack with about 10 grams of protein can be the difference between a steady afternoon and a 4 p.m. raid on the cookie jar.

%% Miriam keeps a list on the fridge door, written in marker on an index card: *eggs, edamame, yogurt bark, chickpeas*. She's fifty-nine and teaches piano from home, and her afternoons used to be a slow slide from one student to the next, fueled by whatever was in the cookie jar. Now, between the 3 o'clock and the 4 o'clock, she takes five minutes for something from the card. "It isn't willpower," she tells her daughter, who asked about the list. "It's just that the good stuff is already there when I'm hungry."

Snacks aren't a failure of willpower or a diet sin. During the transition they can be genuinely useful: they steady blood sugar between meals, help you reach your daily protein target and take the edge off cravings before they take over. Most of the snacks in this chapter provide about 10 grams of protein, and together with three meals of 25 to 30 grams, they help most women reach the 80 to 100 grams a day that the meal plan aims for.

Every snack here takes 15 minutes or less to make, or can be made ahead in a batch. Each one shows its estimated protein, and the "Soothing Sips" at the end are there for comfort and hydration rather than protein, so pair them with something more substantial if you're hungry.

## Savory Bites

@snack Crispy Roasted Chickpeas, Three Ways
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | Serves 2 | 35 min | Protein ≈10 g
Crunchy, salty and dangerously moreish, these are the answer to the chip craving. Make a batch on Sunday and keep them in a jar for the week.

* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) chickpeas, drained, rinsed and patted very dry
* 1 tbsp olive oil and a pinch of salt
* **Smoky:** 1 tsp smoked paprika and 1/2 tsp garlic powder
* **Za'atar:** 2 tsp za'atar and a little lemon zest
* **Cinnamon-maple:** 1 tsp cinnamon and 1 tsp maple syrup (add for the last 5 minutes)

1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Toss the chickpeas with the oil and salt and spread on a baking sheet.
2. Roast for 25 to 30 minutes, shaking the pan halfway, until golden and crunchy.
3. Toss with your chosen seasoning while hot. Cool completely before storing in an airtight jar for up to 5 days.

**Swap:** any canned beans work, such as cannellini or black beans; the cinnamon-maple version needs certified gluten-free spices if you're avoiding gluten.

**Why it helps:** chickpeas provide plant protein and fiber, which help keep you full and your blood sugar steady.

@snack Chili-Lime Edamame
@info GF | DF | VG | 10 min | Serves 2 | Protein ≈10 g
Warm, salty edamame in their pods, tossed with lime and a little chili. Popping them out of the pods slows you down, which makes them a satisfying snack.

* 2 cups (300 g) frozen edamame in pods
* 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
* Finely grated zest and juice of 1/2 lime
* 1/4 tsp chili flakes
* Flaky salt

1. Cook the edamame in boiling water or the microwave according to the package instructions. Drain well.
2. Toss with the sesame oil, lime zest and juice, chili and a pinch of flaky salt. Eat warm or cold.

**Swap:** leave out the chili if spicy food triggers hot flashes. **Why it helps:** edamame are young soybeans, rich in protein, fiber and isoflavones.

@snack Everything-Seasoned Boiled Eggs
@info GF | DF | V | Make-Ahead | 15 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈12 g
The original grab-and-go snack, made more exciting with a sprinkle of everything-bagel seasoning.

* 2 eggs per serving (boil 6 to 12 at a time)
* Everything-bagel seasoning, or a mix of sesame seeds, poppy seeds, dried garlic, dried onion and flaky salt

1. Lower the eggs into a pan of boiling water and cook for 9 to 10 minutes for firm yolks (7 to 8 minutes for jammy ones).
2. Transfer to a bowl of ice water for 5 minutes, then peel.
3. Store the eggs in the fridge, peeled or in their shells, for up to 1 week. Halve and sprinkle with seasoning to serve.

**Swap:** for an egg-free, vegan option, try Chili-Lime Edamame or Crispy Roasted Chickpeas.

**Why it helps:** eggs provide high-quality protein, vitamin B12 and choline.

@snack Hummus & Rainbow Crudités Box
@info GF | DF | VG | 10 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈10 g
A colorful, crunchy box that's as easy to pack for work as it is to eat at home. Edamame turns it from a nibble into a proper snack.

* 1/3 cup (80 g) hummus
* 1/3 cup (50 g) shelled edamame, thawed
* A rainbow of crunchy vegetables: carrot and cucumber sticks, bell pepper strips, cherry tomatoes, radishes, snap peas

1. Spoon the hummus into a small container and scatter the edamame on top.
2. Pack the vegetables alongside. Keep chilled until ready to eat.

**Swap:** use white bean dip or tzatziki instead of hummus (tzatziki isn't DF or VG).

**Why it helps:** chickpeas and edamame provide plant protein and fiber, and the raw vegetables add vitamin C and crunch.

@snack White Bean & Rosemary Dip
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 10 min | Serves 4 | Protein ≈10 g
A creamy, garlicky dip that's lighter than hummus and takes five minutes in a food processor. Serve with Seeded Flax Crackers or vegetable sticks.

* 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
* 2 tbsp tahini
* 2 tbsp olive oil, plus extra to drizzle
* Juice of 1 lemon
* 1 small garlic clove
* 1 tsp chopped fresh rosemary
* Salt and black pepper

1. Blend everything in a food processor until smooth, adding a splash of water if needed.
2. Spoon into a bowl, drizzle with olive oil and keep in the fridge for up to 4 days.

**Swap:** no tahini? Use 2 tbsp Greek yogurt (not DF or VG) or an extra tablespoon of olive oil.

**Why it helps:** white beans bring plant protein, fiber and potassium. Served with crackers, a quarter of the dip makes a satisfying snack.

@snack Smoked Salmon Cucumber Bites
@info GF | 10 min | Serves 2 | Protein ≈12 g
Elegant enough for guests and easy enough for a Tuesday afternoon.

* 1 large cucumber, cut into thick rounds
* 1/4 cup (60 g) whipped cream cheese or Greek yogurt
* 4 oz (115 g) smoked salmon, torn into pieces
* Fresh dill, capers and black pepper

1. Spread each cucumber round with a little cream cheese.
2. Top with smoked salmon, a sprig of dill, a caper or two and a twist of black pepper.

**Swap:** use dairy-free cream cheese for a DF version. **Why it helps:** salmon provides protein, omega-3s and vitamin D.

@snack Seeded Flax Crackers
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 1 hr 10 min | Makes about 30 | Protein ≈10 g with topping
Crisp, nutty crackers made almost entirely from seeds. They keep for two weeks in an airtight container and pair with every dip in this chapter.

* 1/2 cup (60 g) ground flaxseed
* 1/2 cup (70 g) pumpkin seeds
* 1/2 cup (70 g) sunflower seeds
* 1/4 cup (40 g) sesame seeds
* 2 tbsp chia seeds
* 1/2 tsp salt
* 3/4 cup (180 ml) water

1. Heat the oven to 325°F (160°C). Mix everything in a bowl and let it stand for 10 minutes, until it forms a thick paste.
2. Spread very thinly between two sheets of parchment, about 1/8 inch (3 mm) thick, then remove the top sheet and score into squares.
3. Bake for 50 to 60 minutes, until crisp and dry. Cool, then break along the score lines.

**Swap:** use any mix of seeds you have; the flaxseed is what holds them together, so keep that.

**Serving idea:** 4 crackers with 1/4 cup (60 g) cottage cheese or a quarter of the White Bean & Rosemary Dip gives about 10 grams of protein. **Why it helps:** seeds provide fiber, magnesium, plant omega-3s and lignans.

@snack Baked Kale Chips
@info GF | DF | VG | 25 min | Serves 2 | Protein ≈8 g
Crisp, savory and surprisingly addictive. Hemp seeds and nutritional yeast add a cheesy flavor and a protein boost.

* 1 large bunch (about 8 oz / 225 g) curly kale, stems removed, torn into pieces
* 1 tbsp olive oil
* 2 tbsp nutritional yeast
* 2 tbsp hemp seeds
* A pinch of salt and smoked paprika

1. Heat the oven to 300°F (150°C). Wash and dry the kale very thoroughly.
2. Massage the kale with the oil, then toss with the nutritional yeast, hemp seeds, salt and paprika.
3. Spread in a single layer on two baking sheets and bake for 15 to 20 minutes, until crisp but still green. Eat the same day.

**Swap:** use pumpkin seeds instead of hemp seeds, or Parmesan instead of nutritional yeast (not DF or VG).

**Why it helps:** kale brings calcium, vitamin K and fiber, and hemp seeds add protein and healthy fats.

@snack Mini Caprese Skewers
@info GF | V | 10 min | Serves 2 | Protein ≈12 g
A little taste of summer on a stick.

* 4 oz (115 g) mozzarella pearls (bocconcini)
* 1 cup (150 g) cherry tomatoes
* Fresh basil leaves
* 1 tsp olive oil and 1 tsp balsamic glaze
* Flaky salt and black pepper

1. Thread a tomato, a basil leaf and a mozzarella ball onto small skewers or toothpicks.
2. Drizzle with olive oil and balsamic glaze, and season with salt and pepper.

**Swap:** for DF and VG, use cubes of marinated tofu or dairy-free mozzarella.

**Why it helps:** mozzarella provides protein and calcium, and tomatoes add vitamin C and lycopene.

@snack Tamari-Roasted Almonds
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 15 min | Serves 6 | Protein ≈7 g
Salty, toasty almonds to keep in your bag for the moments when hunger hits away from home.

* 1 1/2 cups (210 g) raw almonds
* 1 tbsp gluten-free tamari
* 1/2 tsp smoked paprika (optional)

1. Heat the oven to 350°F (180°C). Toss the almonds with the tamari and paprika.
2. Spread on a lined baking sheet and roast for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring once, until fragrant. Cool completely; they'll crisp as they cool.
3. Store in an airtight jar for up to 2 weeks. A serving is 1/4 cup (35 g).

**Swap:** use regular soy sauce if you don't need it gluten-free, or cashews or pecans for the almonds.

**Why it helps:** almonds bring protein, healthy fats, magnesium, calcium and vitamin E. Pair with a piece of fruit or a cheese stick for more protein.

@snack Cottage Cheese & Cherry Tomato Bowl
@info GF | V | 5 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈13 g
Cottage cheese has made a comeback, and for good reason: it's one of the easiest high-protein snacks there is.

* 1/2 cup (115 g) cottage cheese
* A handful of cherry tomatoes, halved
* 1 tsp olive oil
* Black pepper, a pinch of flaky salt and a sprinkle of everything-bagel seasoning or fresh basil

1. Spoon the cottage cheese into a bowl, top with the tomatoes, and drizzle with oil.
2. Season with pepper, salt and seasoning or basil.

**Swap:** for DF and VG, use crumbled firm tofu with a squeeze of lemon, or plain soy yogurt.

**Why it helps:** cottage cheese provides plenty of protein and some calcium; tomatoes add vitamin C.

## Sweet Treats

@snack Apple Slices with Almond Butter & Cinnamon
@info GF | DF | VG | 5 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈10 g
Crisp, sweet apple with a creamy, cinnamon-spiced dip. It tastes like dessert and works like a snack.

* 1 apple, cored and sliced
* 2 tbsp almond butter
* 1 tbsp hemp seeds
* A pinch of ground cinnamon

1. Fan the apple slices on a plate.
2. Spoon the almond butter alongside or drizzle it over, then sprinkle with hemp seeds and cinnamon.

**Swap:** use peanut or sunflower seed butter (nut-free), and pear instead of apple.

**Why it helps:** almond butter and hemp seeds add protein and healthy fats, which slow the release of the apple's natural sugar.

@snack Walnut-Date Energy Bites
@info GF option | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 15 min + chilling | Makes 16 | Protein ≈8 g per 3 bites
Fudgy, no-bake bites that taste like a treat and keep for a week in the fridge.

* 1 cup (150 g) soft pitted dates
* 1 cup (100 g) walnuts
* 1/2 cup (45 g) rolled oats
* 3 tbsp natural peanut butter
* 3 tbsp hemp seeds
* 2 tbsp ground flaxseed
* 1 tbsp cocoa powder and a pinch of salt

1. Blend the dates in a food processor until they form a paste. Add everything else and pulse until the mixture sticks together.
2. Roll into 16 balls and chill for at least 30 minutes. Keep in the fridge for up to a week, or freeze.

**Swap:** use certified gluten-free oats for GF. **Why it helps:** walnuts and flaxseed provide plant omega-3s, and the nut butter and hemp seeds add protein.

@snack Berry Greek Yogurt Bark
@info GF | V | Make-Ahead | 10 min + freezing | Serves 4 | Protein ≈11 g
A frozen, crackable slab of yogurt studded with berries and dark chocolate. It's cooling on a warm afternoon, too.

* 2 cups (450 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 1 tbsp honey or maple syrup
* 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
* 1 cup (150 g) mixed berries
* 2 tbsp chopped dark chocolate and 2 tbsp chopped pistachios

1. Line a baking sheet with parchment. Mix the yogurt, honey and vanilla, then spread about 1/2 inch (1 cm) thick on the sheet.
2. Scatter over the berries, chocolate and pistachios.
3. Freeze for at least 3 hours, then break into pieces. Store in the freezer and let soften for 5 minutes before eating.

**Swap:** for DF and VG, use thick unsweetened soy yogurt and maple syrup, and check the chocolate is dairy-free.

**Why it helps:** Greek yogurt provides protein and calcium, and berries add fiber and antioxidants.

@snack Vanilla Chia Pudding Cups
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 5 min + chilling | Serves 1 | Protein ≈12 g
Make a few jars at once and you have a creamy, ready-made snack for the next three days.

* 1 cup (240 ml) unsweetened soy milk
* 3 tbsp chia seeds
* 1 tsp maple syrup
* 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
* Fresh fruit and a few chopped nuts, to serve

1. Stir the soy milk, chia, maple syrup and vanilla together in a jar. Stir again after 5 minutes to break up any clumps.
2. Refrigerate for at least 2 hours or overnight. Top with fruit and nuts to serve. Keeps for 3 days.

**Swap:** use any unsweetened plant or dairy milk; soy and dairy milk give the most protein.

**Why it helps:** soy milk provides protein and calcium, and chia seeds add fiber and plant omega-3s.

@snack Dark Chocolate & Almond Clusters
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 15 min + setting | Makes 12 | Protein ≈5 g per 2 clusters
Proof that a menopause-friendly kitchen has room for chocolate. These are a treat, so enjoy them slowly.

* 3 1/2 oz (100 g) dairy-free dark chocolate (70% or higher), chopped
* 1 cup (140 g) toasted almonds, roughly chopped
* 2 tbsp pumpkin seeds
* Flaky salt

1. Melt the chocolate gently in a heatproof bowl over simmering water, or in the microwave in 20-second bursts.
2. Stir in the almonds and pumpkin seeds. Drop 12 spoonfuls onto a lined tray and sprinkle with flaky salt.
3. Chill for 20 minutes until set. Keep in the fridge for up to 2 weeks.

**Swap:** use any nuts or seeds you like. Many dark chocolates contain milk or are made on shared lines, so choose one labeled dairy-free or vegan if you need it to be.

**Why it helps:** dark chocolate and almonds both contain magnesium, and the nuts add protein and healthy fats. Pair with a glass of milk or soy milk for about 8 grams more protein.

@snack Frozen Yogurt-Dipped Blueberries
@info GF | V | Make-Ahead | 10 min + freezing | Serves 2 | Protein ≈11 g
Little frozen bites that pop in your mouth. They're especially welcome when you're feeling too warm.

* 1 1/2 cups (225 g) blueberries
* 1 cup (225 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 1 tsp honey

1. Line a tray with parchment. Stir the honey into the yogurt.
2. Using a toothpick or fork, dip each blueberry into the yogurt and place on the tray. (Or stir all the berries in and drop small clusters onto the tray.)
3. Freeze for 1 to 2 hours, until firm. Store in a freezer bag.

**Swap:** for DF and VG, use thick unsweetened soy yogurt and maple syrup.

**Why it helps:** blueberries bring fiber and antioxidants, and Greek yogurt provides protein and calcium.

@snack Banana-Oat Mini Muffins
@info GF option | V | Make-Ahead | 25 min | Makes 24 | Protein ≈5 g per 2 muffins
Soft, gently sweet mini muffins made without refined flour. Great for lunchboxes, and they freeze well.

* 2 ripe bananas, mashed
* 2 eggs
* 3/4 cup (170 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 2 tbsp maple syrup
* 1 tsp vanilla extract
* 2 cups (180 g) rolled oats, blended into a coarse flour
* 1 tsp baking powder and 1 tsp cinnamon
* 1/2 cup (60 g) chopped walnuts or blueberries

1. Heat the oven to 350°F (180°C) and grease a 24-cup mini muffin tin.
2. Whisk the bananas, eggs, yogurt, maple syrup and vanilla. Stir in the oat flour, baking powder and cinnamon, then the walnuts or blueberries.
3. Spoon into the tin and bake for 12 to 15 minutes, until risen and golden. Cool before storing.

**Swap:** use certified gluten-free oats for GF. **Why it helps:** oats provide fiber, and the eggs and yogurt add protein. Have two with a glass of milk for a more substantial snack.

@snack Baked Cinnamon Pear with Walnuts
@info GF | V | 25 min | Serves 2 | Protein ≈12 g
A warm, cozy evening snack that tastes like dessert and helps you wind down.

* 2 ripe pears, halved and cored
* 1 tsp maple syrup
* 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
* 2 tbsp chopped walnuts
* 1 cup (225 g) plain Greek yogurt, to serve

1. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Place the pears cut side up in a small baking dish.
2. Drizzle with maple syrup, sprinkle with cinnamon and walnuts, and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, until soft.
3. Serve warm with a generous spoonful of Greek yogurt.

**Swap:** use soy yogurt for DF and VG. **Why it helps:** pears provide fiber, walnuts add omega-3s, and the yogurt brings protein and calcium.

@snack Seeded Rye Crispbread with Ricotta & Pear
@info V | 5 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈12 g
Creamy ricotta, sweet pear and a drizzle of honey on crunchy rye: a snack that feels like a treat.

* 2 seeded rye crispbreads
* 1/3 cup (80 g) part-skim ricotta
* 1/2 pear, thinly sliced
* 1 tsp honey and a few chopped pistachios

1. Spread the ricotta over the crispbreads.
2. Top with pear slices, a drizzle of honey and the pistachios.

**Swap:** use gluten-free crispbread for GF, and dairy-free cream cheese or hummus for DF and VG.

**Why it helps:** ricotta provides protein and calcium, and rye adds fiber.

@snack Spiced Pumpkin Seed Trail Mix
@info GF | DF | VG | Make-Ahead | 10 min | Serves 8 | Protein ≈8 g
A handful of this keeps in your bag, your desk drawer or your car for those moments when you need something now.

* 1 cup (130 g) pumpkin seeds
* 1/2 cup (70 g) almonds
* 1/2 cup (60 g) walnut halves
* 1/3 cup (50 g) unsweetened dried cherries or raisins
* 1 tsp olive oil, 1/2 tsp cinnamon, 1/4 tsp ground ginger and a pinch of salt

1. Heat the oven to 325°F (160°C). Toss the seeds and nuts with the oil and spices.
2. Roast for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring once. Cool, then mix in the dried fruit.
3. Store in an airtight jar for up to 2 weeks. A serving is 1/4 cup (35 g).

**Swap:** for a nut-free version, use sunflower seeds instead of almonds and walnuts.

**Why it helps:** pumpkin seeds are one of the best sources of magnesium, and also provide protein, zinc and iron.

## Soothing Sips

@snack Tart Cherry Evening Spritz
@info GF | DF | VG | 5 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈1 g
A grown-up, alcohol-free drink for the evening, with a deep ruby color and a sweet-tart flavor.

* 1/4 cup (60 ml) unsweetened tart cherry juice
* 3/4 cup (180 ml) sparkling water
* Ice, a squeeze of lime and a sprig of mint

1. Fill a glass with ice. Add the cherry juice, top with sparkling water and finish with lime and mint.

**Swap:** use pomegranate juice instead of cherry, or add a few frozen cherries as ice cubes.

**Why it helps:** tart cherries contain small amounts of natural melatonin, and this makes a lovely swap for an evening glass of wine.

@snack Golden Turmeric Latte
@info GF | DF | VG | 10 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈8 g
A warm, golden, caffeine-free mug to wrap your hands around in the evening.

* 1 cup (240 ml) unsweetened soy milk
* 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
* 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon
* A pinch of ground ginger and a pinch of black pepper
* 1 tsp maple syrup (or honey if you're not vegan)

1. Warm everything in a small pan over medium heat, whisking until steaming and frothy. Don't let it boil.
2. Pour into a mug and sprinkle with a little extra cinnamon.

**Swap:** any milk works; dairy milk keeps the protein the same (but isn't dairy-free), while oat or almond milk will lower it.

**Why it helps:** soy milk provides protein and calcium, and the spices add warmth without caffeine.

@snack Cucumber-Mint Cooling Water
@info GF | DF | VG | 5 min | Makes 1 pitcher | Protein 0 g
Keep a pitcher in the fridge for hot days and hot flashes.

* 1/2 cucumber, thinly sliced
* A handful of fresh mint leaves
* 1 lime, sliced
* 8 cups (2 liters) cold water and plenty of ice

1. Put everything in a large pitcher and refrigerate for at least an hour. Top up with water through the day.

**Swap:** try strawberry and basil, or lemon and ginger.

**Why it helps:** it makes drinking enough water more inviting, which matters when night sweats and hot flashes leave you depleted.

@snack Kefir Berry Shake
@info GF | V | 5 min | Serves 1 | Protein ≈14 g
Tangy, creamy and full of live cultures, ready in the time it takes to rinse the blender.

* 1 cup (240 ml) plain kefir
* 1/4 cup (60 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 3/4 cup (110 g) frozen mixed berries
* 1 tsp honey (optional)

1. Blend everything until smooth. Drink straight away.

**Swap:** for DF and VG, use unsweetened soy milk and soy yogurt instead of kefir and Greek yogurt.

**Why it helps:** kefir contains live cultures that support gut health, and together with Greek yogurt it provides protein and calcium.
