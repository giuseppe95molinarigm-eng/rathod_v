## The Yoga Poses

### 1. Mountain Pose

1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart, weight spread evenly through both feet.
2. Lengthen up through the crown of your head; relax your shoulders down and back.
3. Let your arms hang by your sides, palms facing forward. Breathe slowly for 5 to 10 breaths.

**Benefits:** posture, balance and body awareness. It's the starting point for every standing pose. **Easier:** stand with your back against a wall. **Harder:** close your eyes (near a wall).

### 2. Easy Seated Pose

1. Sit cross-legged on a folded blanket so your hips are higher than your knees, or sit on a chair with your feet flat.
2. Lengthen your spine and rest your hands on your thighs.
3. Close your eyes or soften your gaze and breathe.

**Benefits:** a stable, comfortable position for breathing practices and meditation. **Easier:** sit on a chair or with your back against a wall.

### 3. Cat-Cow

1. Come onto your hands and knees, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
2. **Cow:** breathe in, let your belly lower and lift your chest and gaze gently.
3. **Cat:** breathe out, round your back gently and let your head relax down.
4. Move slowly with your breath for 6 to 8 rounds.

**Benefits:** gently mobilizes the spine and coordinates movement with breath. **Easier:** do it seated on a chair, hands on your knees. **Bone-safe:** with osteoporosis, emphasize Cow (lengthening) and keep Cat small, or skip the rounding and simply lengthen your spine.

### 4. Child's Pose

1. From hands and knees, bring your big toes together and widen your knees.
2. Sit your hips back toward your heels and rest your forehead on the floor, a block or a cushion.
3. Stretch your arms forward or rest them by your sides. Breathe into your back for 5 to 10 breaths.

**Benefits:** a restful pose that releases the lower back and calms the mind. **Easier:** put a pillow between your heels and hips, or a bolster under your chest. **Bone-safe:** support your chest with a bolster or pillows so your spine stays long.

### 5. Downward-Facing Dog

1. From hands and knees, tuck your toes and lift your hips up and back, forming an upside-down V.
2. Bend your knees as much as you need to keep your back long.
3. Press the floor away with your hands and breathe for 5 breaths.

**Benefits:** strengthens the arms and shoulders and stretches the back of the legs. **Easier:** do it with your hands on the seat of a chair or on a wall. **Caution:** skip it if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure or glaucoma.

### 6. Low Lunge

1. From hands and knees, step your right foot forward between your hands, knee over ankle.
2. Lower your left knee to a folded blanket and lift your chest, hands on your front thigh or on blocks.
3. Gently move your hips forward to feel a stretch at the front of the left hip. Hold for 5 breaths, then switch sides.

**Benefits:** stretches the hip flexors, which tighten with sitting. **Easier:** do it standing, holding a chair, with your back knee bent.

### 7. Warrior I

1. Stand with your feet about a leg's length apart, front foot pointing forward, back foot turned out slightly.
2. Bend your front knee over your ankle and keep your back leg strong.
3. Square your hips forward and reach your arms up, or rest your hands on your hips. Hold for 5 breaths, then switch sides.

**Benefits:** strengthens the legs and opens the chest and hips. **Easier:** take a shorter stance and hold a chair. **Harder:** sink deeper into the front knee.

### 8. Warrior II

1. Stand with your feet wide apart, right foot turned out, left foot slightly in.
2. Bend your right knee over your ankle and stretch your arms out to the sides at shoulder height.
3. Look over your right fingers and breathe for 5 breaths. Switch sides.

**Benefits:** builds strength and stamina in the legs and focus in the mind. **Easier:** shorten your stance and bend your knee less.

### 9. Triangle Pose (Supported)

1. From a wide stance, turn your right foot out and keep both legs straight but not locked.
2. Reach your right arm forward, then hinge sideways from your hip and rest your right hand on your shin, a block or the seat of a chair.
3. Reach your left arm up and keep your spine long. Hold for 5 breaths, then switch sides.

**Benefits:** stretches the sides of the body and the legs, and builds balance. **Bone-safe:** keep your hand high (on a chair) and your spine long; don't round or twist forcefully.

### 10. Tree Pose

1. Stand near a wall or chair. Shift your weight onto your left foot.
2. Place your right foot on your left ankle, calf or inner thigh (never on the knee).
3. Bring your hands together at your chest or lift them overhead. Hold for 5 to 8 breaths, then switch sides.

**Benefits:** improves balance, which lowers your risk of falls. **Easier:** keep your toes on the floor like a kickstand, and hold the wall. **Harder:** close your eyes, near the wall.

### 11. Chair Pose

1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart.
2. Bend your knees and sit your hips back as if sitting into a chair, keeping your chest lifted.
3. Reach your arms forward or up. Hold for 3 to 5 breaths.

**Benefits:** strengthens the thighs and buttocks. **Easier:** hover just above a real chair, or slide down a wall.

### 12. Goddess Pose

1. Stand with your feet wide, toes turned out.
2. Bend your knees over your toes and lower your hips, keeping your spine tall.
3. Bring your arms out with elbows bent, palms forward. Hold for 5 breaths.

**Benefits:** strengthens the legs and inner thighs and opens the hips. **Easier:** bend your knees less, and hold a chair in front of you.

### 13. Bridge Pose

1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat, hip-width apart.
2. Press into your feet and lift your hips, keeping your knees in line with your hips.
3. Hold for 3 to 5 breaths, then lower slowly. Repeat 3 times.

**Benefits:** strengthens the buttocks, back of the legs and back muscles. **Easier:** lift only a little. **Bone-safe:** a good choice; keep your neck relaxed and don't push your weight onto it.

### 14. Sphinx Pose

1. Lie on your front with your elbows under your shoulders and forearms on the floor.
2. Press your forearms down and gently lift your chest, lengthening your spine.
3. Keep your shoulders relaxed and breathe for 5 to 8 breaths.

**Benefits:** gently strengthens and lengthens the back. **Bone-safe:** an excellent spine-friendly pose.

### 15. Low Cobra

1. Lie on your front with your hands under your shoulders.
2. Press your hips down and lift your chest a few inches, using your back muscles more than your arms.
3. Hold for 3 breaths, lower, and repeat 3 times.

**Benefits:** strengthens the back and opens the chest. **Easier:** stay in Sphinx Pose.

### 16. Locust Pose

1. Lie on your front with your arms by your sides, palms down, forehead on the floor.
2. Breathe in and lift your chest, arms and legs a little way off the floor, keeping your neck long.
3. Hold for 3 breaths, then lower. Repeat 3 times.

**Benefits:** strengthens the muscles along the spine, which support good posture. **Easier:** lift only your chest, or only your legs. **Bone-safe:** a valuable pose for spinal strength; lift only as high as feels comfortable.

### 17. Bird-Dog

1. Come onto your hands and knees with a long, flat back.
2. Reach your right arm forward and your left leg back, keeping your hips level.
3. Hold for 3 breaths, then switch sides. Repeat 4 to 6 times each side.

**Benefits:** strengthens the core and back and improves balance. **Easier:** extend only the leg, or only the arm. **Bone-safe:** keep your back flat, not arched or rounded.

### 18. Gentle Supine Twist

1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat.
2. Let your knees lower slowly to the right, only as far as is comfortable, and turn your head to the left if that feels good.
3. Breathe for 5 breaths, then bring your knees back to center and switch sides.

**Benefits:** releases tension in the back and hips. **Bone-safe:** keep the twist small and supported with a pillow under your knees.

### 19. Reclined Bound Angle

1. Lie on your back and bring the soles of your feet together, letting your knees fall open.
2. Support each knee with a pillow or rolled blanket.
3. Rest your hands on your belly and breathe for 1 to 3 minutes.

**Benefits:** a deeply relaxing pose that gently opens the hips. **Easier:** put a pillow under your head and keep your feet further from your hips.

### 20. Legs Up the Wall

1. Sit sideways next to a wall, then swing your legs up the wall as you lie back.
2. Shuffle your hips as close to the wall as is comfortable; bend your knees if your hamstrings are tight.
3. Rest your arms by your sides and breathe for 2 to 5 minutes.

**Benefits:** restful and calming, and many women find it eases tired legs. **Caution:** skip it if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure or glaucoma; instead, rest your calves on the seat of a chair.

### 21. Supported Chest Opener

1. Place a rolled blanket or firm pillow lengthwise behind you and lie back on it so it supports your spine from mid-back to head.
2. Let your arms rest out to the sides, palms up.
3. Breathe slowly for 1 to 3 minutes.

**Benefits:** opens the chest and counteracts hours of sitting and screen time. **Bone-safe:** a gentle, supported extension pose.

### 22. Seated Side Bend

1. Sit tall on a chair or cross-legged on a blanket.
2. Reach your right arm up and lean gently to the left, keeping both sitting bones down.
3. Hold for 3 to 5 breaths, then switch sides.

**Benefits:** stretches the sides of the body and helps breathing feel fuller.

### 23. Hip Hinge with Chair

1. Stand facing a chair, about an arm's length away.
2. Keeping your spine long and flat, hinge from your hips and rest your hands on the chair back.
3. Let your back lengthen and your hamstrings stretch. Hold for 5 breaths, then walk your hands up and stand.

**Benefits:** stretches the back of the legs and teaches the bone-safe way to bend. **Bone-safe:** use this in place of any forward fold.

### 24. Reclined Hamstring Stretch with Strap

1. Lie on your back and loop a strap or scarf around the sole of your right foot.
2. Straighten your right leg toward the ceiling, holding the strap, and keep your left knee bent with the foot on the floor.
3. Hold for 5 to 8 breaths, then switch sides.

**Benefits:** a safe way to stretch the hamstrings without bending your spine.

### 25. Corpse Pose (Relaxation Pose)

1. Lie on your back with your legs extended and arms by your sides, palms up. Put a pillow under your knees and head if you like.
2. Close your eyes and let your body feel heavy.
3. Rest for 3 to 10 minutes, or for the length of your meditation script.

**Benefits:** deep rest that lets your body absorb the practice. **Easier:** lie on your side, or rest your calves on a chair.

## The Strength Moves

Do each move slowly and with control, breathing out as you make the effort. Start with the repetitions for your week (Chapter 19) and use the easier option whenever you need it.

### Strength 1. Sit-to-Stand

1. Sit near the front of a sturdy chair, feet hip-width apart.
2. Lean forward slightly with a long spine and stand up, pressing through your heels.
3. Slowly sit back down, taking 2 to 3 seconds. That's one repetition.

**Easier:** use your hands on your thighs or a higher seat. **Harder:** cross your arms over your chest, or hold a can or water bottle at your chest.

### Strength 2. Wall Push-Up

1. Stand an arm's length from a wall and place your hands on it at shoulder height.
2. Keeping your body in a straight line, bend your elbows and bring your chest toward the wall.
3. Push back to the start.

**Easier:** stand closer to the wall. **Harder:** use a kitchen counter or a sturdy table, which puts more weight through your arms.

### Strength 3. Glute Bridge

1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat.
2. Press into your heels and lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees.
3. Pause, then lower slowly.

**Harder:** hold the top for 3 seconds, or lift one foot slightly off the floor. **Bone-safe:** a great choice for strengthening the hips and back.

### Strength 4. Step-Up

1. Stand facing the bottom stair, holding the rail.
2. Step up with your right foot and press through it to bring your left foot up.
3. Step down with control. Do all your repetitions on one side, then switch.

**Easier:** use a lower step. **Harder:** hold a can or bottle in your free hand.

### Strength 5. Supported Split Squat

1. Stand side-on to a chair or counter and hold it with one hand. Step one foot back into a staggered stance.
2. Bend both knees, lowering your back knee toward the floor, keeping your chest tall.
3. Press through your front foot to rise. Do all your repetitions, then switch legs.

**Easier:** make the movement smaller. **Harder:** go a little lower, or hold a weight in your free hand.

### Strength 6. Calf Raise and Heel Drop

1. Stand tall holding the back of a chair.
2. Rise up onto the balls of your feet, then lower your heels with control.
3. **For bone loading (if your doctor agrees):** after each raise, let your heels drop to the floor with a small, firm thump.

**Benefits:** strengthens the calves and ankles for balance, and heel drops add a gentle impact that bones respond to. **Bone-safe:** if you have osteoporosis or a previous fracture, check with your doctor before adding heel drops.

### Strength 7. Bent-Over Row

1. Hold a can or water bottle in your right hand and rest your left hand on a chair or counter.
2. Hinge forward from your hips with a long, flat spine, knees soft.
3. Pull the weight up toward your ribs, squeezing your shoulder blade back, then lower slowly. Switch sides after your repetitions.

**Easier:** use a resistance band looped around a door handle and pull while standing upright. **Bone-safe:** keep your back flat, never rounded.

### Strength 8. Farmer's Carry

1. Hold a heavy shopping bag, can or water bottle in each hand, arms by your sides.
2. Stand tall with your shoulders relaxed and walk slowly for 30 to 60 seconds.
3. Rest, then repeat.

**Benefits:** builds grip, shoulder and core strength, and makes carrying groceries easier in real life. **Harder:** carry heavier bags, or walk for longer.

## Breathing Practices

None of these practices is a treatment for hot flashes. They're tools for calming your nervous system, easing stress and helping you sleep, and many women find they help them feel steadier.

### The Longer Out-Breath

Sit or lie comfortably. Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4, then out through your nose or softly parted lips for a count of 6. Continue for 2 to 5 minutes. A longer exhale switches on the body's "rest and digest" response, which is why this is the practice to reach for in a moment of stress.

### Belly Breathing

Lie down with one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Breathe in slowly through your nose and let your belly rise under your hand while your chest stays fairly still. Breathe out and feel your belly fall. Continue for 3 to 5 minutes.

### Cooling Breath

Sit comfortably. Curl your tongue into a tube (if you can't, rest the tip of your tongue behind your top teeth and breathe in through your teeth). Breathe in slowly through your mouth, feeling the cool air. Close your mouth and breathe out slowly through your nose. Repeat for 1 to 3 minutes. It's a calming, refreshing practice for warm days or tense moments. It won't treat hot flashes, but many women find it soothing.

### Alternate Nostril Breathing

Sit tall. Gently close your right nostril with your right thumb and breathe in through the left. Close the left nostril with your ring finger, release the right and breathe out through the right. Breathe in through the right, close it, and breathe out through the left. That's one round. Continue for 2 to 4 minutes, breathing smoothly, without holding your breath.

### Counting Breaths

Sit or lie comfortably and simply count each out-breath, from 1 to 10, then start again at 1. If you lose count, which everyone does, gently begin again. It's a simple way to steady a busy mind.

## Meditation Scripts

Read these slowly to yourself, pausing between sentences, or record them on your phone in your own voice and play them back.

### Script 1: Body Scan (5 to 10 minutes)

*Lie down comfortably and close your eyes. Take three slow breaths, letting each out-breath be a little longer than the in-breath. Bring your attention to your feet. Notice whatever is there: warmth, coolness, tingling, or nothing at all. There's no right thing to feel. Let your feet soften. Move your attention slowly up through your ankles, your calves, your knees and your thighs, letting each area grow heavy and relaxed. Notice your hips and your lower back, resting on the floor. Feel your belly rising and falling with your breath. Bring your attention to your chest, your shoulders, your arms and your hands, letting them soften. Notice your neck, your jaw, your face and the space between your eyebrows, and let them relax. Now feel your whole body, from head to toe, resting and breathing. Stay here for a few breaths. When you're ready, wiggle your fingers and toes, and slowly open your eyes.*

### Script 2: Loving-Kindness (5 minutes)

*Sit comfortably and close your eyes. Place a hand on your heart if that feels good. Silently repeat: May I be safe. May I be well. May I be kind to myself. May I meet this time of change with patience. Let the words settle. Now bring to mind someone you love, and wish them the same: May you be safe. May you be well. May you be happy. Then think of someone you find difficult, and, if you can, wish them well too. Finally, widen your wish to all the women going through this transition with you: May we all be safe. May we all be well. Rest for a few breaths before opening your eyes.*

### Script 3: Gratitude (5 minutes)

*Sit or lie comfortably and take a few slow breaths. Think of one thing from today that you're grateful for, however small: a warm drink, a kind word, a moment of quiet. Picture it clearly and notice how it feels in your body. Now think of something your body did for you today: carried you, healed, breathed, held someone. Thank it, silently. Finally, think of one thing you're looking forward to. Rest with that feeling for a few breaths, then gently open your eyes.*

### Script 4: Sleep Relaxation (10 minutes)

*Lie in bed or on the floor, warm and comfortable. Let your eyes close and your breath slow down. With each out-breath, imagine your body sinking a little deeper into the surface beneath you. Silently say to yourself: My feet are heavy and warm. My legs are heavy and warm. My arms are heavy and warm. My breath is slow and easy. Picture a calm place you know well, perhaps a beach, a garden or a quiet room, and notice its colors, sounds and smells. Rest there. If thoughts come, let them drift past like clouds, and return to your calm place. There's nothing you need to do now. You can let go of the day. If you're in bed, allow yourself to drift into sleep.*

### Script 5: Five Senses Grounding (3 minutes)

*Wherever you are, pause. Notice five things you can see. Four things you can feel, like your feet on the floor or your clothes on your skin. Three things you can hear. Two things you can smell. One thing you can taste. Take one slow breath. You're here, and you're all right.* This is especially useful in moments of anxiety or overwhelm.
