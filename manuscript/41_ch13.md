@chapter 13 | Breakfasts
> For many women, breakfast is the meal with the least protein, and the one where adding some can help the most.

%% Ruth was sixty-three and had stopped bothering with breakfast after her husband died. Cooking for one felt pointless, and a cup of tea with a plain cracker got her to lunch, more or less. Then the physical therapist treating her stiff knee asked what she ate in the mornings, and frowned at the answer. "Your muscles are hungry," she said. Ruth started small: a boiled egg and a pot of yogurt, eaten at the table by the window instead of standing at the counter. It wasn't dramatic. But for the first time in a long while, breakfast felt like something she did for herself.

That's why breakfast comes first in this book.

Breakfast is where Anchor, Add, Adapt makes its biggest difference. A morning meal with around 20 to 30 grams of protein, plenty of fiber and a little healthy fat keeps your blood sugar steady and quiets the mid-morning cravings. It also starts protecting your muscles before the day has properly begun. Most of these recipes can be made ahead, because nobody has time to cook at 7 a.m. on a Wednesday.

## How the Recipes Work

Every recipe follows the same layout. The **labels** under each title tell you whether it's gluten-free (GF), dairy-free (DF), vegetarian (V) or vegan (VG), or can easily be made so ("option"). **Swaps & Variations** shows you how to adapt it to your needs or to what's in your fridge, and **Why It Helps** explains what it does for you during the transition.

@recipe Golden Flax & Berry Overnight Oats
@tags V | VG option | GF option | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook None | Keeps 3 days | Protein ≈20 g
This is the answer to the 6:45 a.m. scramble. Five minutes the night before, while the kettle boils, and breakfast is waiting in the fridge like a favor from your past self. Cool, creamy oats, berries bleeding purple into the yogurt, and a warm hint of cinnamon. Greek yogurt and soy milk do the anchoring, and ground flaxseed slips in fiber and omega-3s without you noticing.

### Ingredients

* 1 cup (90 g) rolled oats
* 2 tbsp ground flaxseed
* 1 tbsp chia seeds
* 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
* 1/4 tsp ground turmeric (optional, for a golden color)
* 1 cup (240 ml) unsweetened soy milk or milk of your choice
* 3/4 cup (170 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 1 to 2 tsp maple syrup or honey (optional)
* 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
* 1 cup (150 g) mixed berries, fresh or frozen
* 2 tbsp chopped walnuts or almonds, to serve

### Method

1. In a bowl or large jar, stir together the oats, flaxseed, chia seeds, cinnamon and turmeric.
2. Add the milk, yogurt, maple syrup (if using) and vanilla. Stir well, making sure there are no dry pockets of oats.
3. Divide between two jars or containers. Top each with half the berries. If using frozen berries, they will thaw overnight and release their juices.
4. Cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or overnight.
5. In the morning, stir, add a splash more milk if you like a looser texture, and top with the chopped nuts. Eat cold, or warm in the microwave for 1 to 2 minutes.

:::swaps
- **Vegan:** use soy yogurt and maple syrup.
- **Gluten-free:** use certified gluten-free oats.
- **More protein:** stir in an extra 1/4 cup (60 g) Greek yogurt or 1 tbsp of a protein powder you like.
- **Flavors:** try grated apple and cinnamon, mashed banana and peanut butter, or mango and lime zest.
:::

:::why
Oats provide soluble fiber that supports healthy cholesterol and steady energy. Flaxseed adds lignans and plant omega-3s, and the combination of yogurt and soy milk gives you a protein-rich start that keeps you full until lunch.
:::

@recipe Spinach & Feta Egg Muffins
@tags GF | V | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 20 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈20 g
Make a tray on Sunday and you've solved four weekday breakfasts before Monday even starts. They're fluffy, salty little frittatas studded with sweet pepper and spinach, and they taste just as good cold from the fridge, eaten in the car, as they do warm on a plate. Three of them, with a piece of fruit, will carry you comfortably to lunch.

### Ingredients

* Olive oil or cooking spray, for greasing
* 10 eggs
* 1/4 cup (60 ml) milk
* 1/2 cup (113 g) cottage cheese
* 1/4 tsp salt and plenty of black pepper
* 2 cups (60 g) baby spinach, chopped
* 1 small red bell pepper, finely diced
* 3 scallions, thinly sliced
* 1/2 cup (75 g) crumbled feta cheese
* 1/2 cup (75 g) cherry tomatoes, quartered
* 1 tsp dried oregano

### Method

1. Heat the oven to 350°F (180°C). Lightly grease a 12-cup muffin tin with olive oil, or line it with silicone or parchment muffin cases.
2. In a large measuring cup or bowl, whisk the eggs with the milk, cottage cheese, salt and pepper.
3. Divide the spinach, red pepper, scallions, feta and tomatoes evenly between the muffin cups. Sprinkle with the oregano.
4. Pour the egg mixture over the vegetables, filling each cup about three-quarters full.
5. Bake for 18 to 22 minutes, until puffed, golden and just set in the center (a knife inserted should come out clean).
6. Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then run a knife around the edges and lift out.

:::swaps
- **Dairy-free:** use a plant milk and replace the feta with diced avocado added after baking, or a dairy-free cheese.
- **Vegetables:** use any leftover roasted vegetables, mushrooms, zucchini or kale.
- **Make it a meal:** serve with a slice of whole-grain toast and some fruit.
- **Storage:** keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days, or freeze for up to 2 months. Reheat in the microwave for 30 to 45 seconds.
:::

:::why
Eggs provide high-quality protein, vitamin B12 and some vitamin D. Feta adds calcium, and spinach contributes folate and magnesium. Having a protein-rich breakfast ready to go makes it much easier to avoid mid-morning energy crashes.
:::

@recipe Greek Yogurt Power Bowl with Walnuts & Seeds
@tags V | GF option | 10 min
@meta Serves 1 | Prep 10 min | Cook None | Best fresh | Protein ≈25 g
On the mornings when cooking is out of the question, this is the bowl to build standing at the counter: thick, tangy yogurt, whatever fruit is ripe, and a shower of crunchy seeds and nuts. It takes less time than waiting in line for a latte, and it keeps you full far longer. Think of it as a template and change the toppings with the seasons.

### Ingredients

* 1 cup (225 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 1/2 tsp vanilla extract (optional)
* 1/2 cup (75 g) berries or chopped fruit, such as kiwi, mango or pear
* 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
* 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds
* 1 tbsp chopped walnuts
* 2 tbsp low-sugar granola or toasted rolled oats
* A pinch of ground cinnamon
* 1 tsp honey or maple syrup (optional)

### Method

1. Spoon the yogurt into a bowl and stir in the vanilla, if using.
2. Top with the fruit, flaxseed, pumpkin seeds, walnuts and granola.
3. Finish with a pinch of cinnamon and a drizzle of honey, if you like. Eat straight away so the toppings stay crunchy.

:::swaps
- **Dairy-free / vegan:** use unsweetened soy yogurt (it has the most protein of the plant yogurts) and maple syrup.
- **Gluten-free:** check that your granola or oats are certified gluten-free.
- **Gut boost:** use half kefir and half yogurt for extra live cultures.
- **To go:** layer in a jar with the toppings in a separate small container.
:::

:::why
Greek yogurt is one of the easiest ways to get both protein and calcium at breakfast. Walnuts and flaxseed provide plant omega-3s, pumpkin seeds add magnesium, and fruit adds fiber and antioxidants.
:::

@recipe Turmeric Tofu & Veggie Scramble
@tags GF | DF | VG | 30 min
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 12 min | Keeps 2 days | Protein ≈25 g
If you've never tried a tofu scramble, this is the one to convert you. Crumbled tofu turns sunny gold with turmeric and soaks up cumin and smoky paprika, while the peppers and mushrooms bring color and bite. It's a plant-based plate that eats like a diner breakfast, and if you pick calcium-set tofu, it's a calcium boost too.

### Ingredients

* 1 tbsp olive oil
* 1/2 small red onion, finely chopped
* 1 small red bell pepper, diced
* 1 cup (70 g) mushrooms, sliced
* 1 block (14 oz / 400 g) firm tofu, drained and patted dry
* 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
* 1/2 tsp ground cumin
* 1/4 tsp smoked paprika
* 1 tbsp nutritional yeast (optional)
* 2 tbsp unsweetened soy milk or water
* 2 large handfuls (60 g) baby spinach
* Salt and black pepper, to taste
* Fresh herbs, such as cilantro or chives, to serve
* Whole-grain or gluten-free toast, to serve (optional)

### Method

1. Heat the olive oil in a large nonstick frying pan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 3 minutes until softening.
2. Add the red pepper and mushrooms and cook for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring now and then, until tender.
3. Crumble the tofu into the pan with your hands, in pieces about the size of scrambled eggs.
4. Sprinkle over the turmeric, cumin, paprika and nutritional yeast (if using). Add the soy milk and stir well to coat the tofu evenly.
5. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring gently, until the tofu is heated through.
6. Add the spinach and stir until just wilted. Season with salt and pepper.
7. Serve topped with fresh herbs, with toast on the side if you like.

:::swaps
- **Calcium boost:** choose tofu labeled "calcium-set" or "made with calcium sulfate."
- **Eggy flavor:** a pinch of kala namak (Indian black salt) at the end gives a surprisingly egg-like taste.
- **Make it a wrap:** spoon into a whole-grain or gluten-free tortilla with avocado and salsa.
- **Spice level:** add chili flakes if you like heat, or leave out if spicy food triggers hot flashes.
:::

:::why
Tofu provides protein and soy isoflavones, which may modestly reduce hot flashes for some women. Turmeric adds anti-inflammatory compounds, and the vegetables contribute fiber, folate and antioxidants.
:::

@recipe Besan Chilla with Mint Yogurt
@tags GF | V | DF option | VG option | 30 min
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 15 min | Best fresh | Protein ≈20 g
Across North India, these savory chickpea-flour pancakes are the breakfast you make when there's "nothing in the house." The batter takes five minutes and the edges crisp up in the pan. Every bite is flecked with onion, tomato, ginger and cilantro. Served with a cool swirl of mint yogurt, they're naturally gluten-free and full of plant protein. They also make a brilliant light lunch.

### Ingredients

#### For the chilla (makes 4)

* 1 cup (100 g) chickpea flour (besan or gram flour)
* 1/2 tsp ground cumin
* 1/4 tsp ground turmeric
* 1/4 tsp salt
* About 3/4 cup (180 ml) water
* 1/2 small red onion, very finely chopped
* 1 small tomato, seeded and finely chopped
* 1 handful (15 g) spinach, finely chopped
* 2 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro
* 1/2 green chili, finely chopped (optional)
* 1/2 tsp grated fresh ginger
* 2 to 3 tsp olive or neutral oil, for cooking

#### For the mint yogurt

* 1 cup (225 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 2 tbsp chopped fresh mint
* A squeeze of lemon juice
* A pinch of salt and ground cumin

### Method

1. In a bowl, whisk the chickpea flour, cumin, turmeric and salt. Gradually whisk in the water until you have a smooth batter about the thickness of heavy cream. Let it rest for 5 minutes.
2. Meanwhile, stir together all the mint yogurt ingredients and set aside.
3. Stir the onion, tomato, spinach, cilantro, chili (if using) and ginger into the batter.
4. Heat a nonstick frying pan over medium heat and brush with a little oil. Pour in a quarter of the batter and quickly spread it into a thin round, about 6 inches (15 cm) across, with the back of a ladle.
5. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until the edges look dry and the underside is golden. Drizzle a few drops of oil around the edge, flip, and cook for another 1 to 2 minutes.
6. Keep warm while you cook the rest, adding a little oil each time.
7. Serve the chilla warm with the mint yogurt.

:::swaps
- **Dairy-free / vegan:** make the mint yogurt with unsweetened soy or coconut yogurt.
- **More protein:** serve with a side of Greek yogurt, a boiled egg or crumbled paneer or tofu stirred into the batter.
- **Mild version:** leave out the chili, especially if spicy food triggers hot flashes.
- **Batter tip:** if the batter thickens as it stands, whisk in a splash more water.
:::

:::why
Chickpea flour provides plant protein, fiber, folate, iron and magnesium, which help keep energy steady. The yogurt adds protein and calcium, and the vegetables and herbs bring color, flavor and antioxidants.
:::

@recipe Green Glow Protein Smoothie
@tags GF | V | DF option | 5 min
@meta Serves 1 | Prep 5 min | Cook None | Best fresh | Protein ≈25 g
For mornings when you wake up already too warm, this frosty, tropical smoothie is pure relief. Frozen mango and banana make it thick and cold, the spinach disappears completely (really), and Greek yogurt and nut butter make it a real meal rather than a sugary drink. Breakfast in a glass, in the time it takes to find your keys.

### Ingredients

* 1 cup (240 ml) unsweetened soy milk or milk
* 1/2 cup (115 g) plain Greek yogurt
* 1 large handful (30 g) baby spinach
* 1/2 small frozen banana
* 1/2 cup (75 g) frozen mango or pineapple chunks
* 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
* 1 tbsp almond or peanut butter
* A small piece of fresh ginger (optional)
* Ice cubes (optional)

### Method

1. Add the milk to a blender first, then the yogurt, spinach, banana, mango, flaxseed, nut butter and ginger (if using).
2. Blend on high for 45 to 60 seconds, until completely smooth. Add a few ice cubes for a thicker, colder smoothie, or a splash more milk to thin it.
3. Pour into a glass and drink straight away.

:::swaps
- **Dairy-free:** use soy yogurt or a scoop of a plant-based protein powder instead of Greek yogurt.
- **Lower sugar:** replace the banana with 1/2 cup (75 g) frozen cauliflower rice. It adds creaminess without changing the flavor.
- **Prep ahead:** portion the spinach, fruit and flaxseed into freezer bags so you just add milk, yogurt and nut butter in the morning.
:::

:::why
Soy milk and Greek yogurt together provide around 20 grams of protein, plus calcium. Spinach adds folate and magnesium, flaxseed adds fiber and omega-3s, and nut butter brings healthy fats to keep you full.
:::

@recipe Smoked Salmon & Avocado Rye Toast
@tags DF | GF option | 15 min
@meta Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 3 min | Best fresh | Protein ≈20 g
A Saturday-morning treat that's quick enough for a Tuesday. Dark, chewy rye, buttery avocado mashed with lemon, silky salmon and a scatter of dill and capers: it feels like brunch at a café, and it's on the table in ten minutes. Salmon brings omega-3s and vitamin D; rye brings the slow, steady energy.

### Ingredients

* 4 slices seeded rye or whole-grain bread
* 1 ripe avocado
* Juice of 1/2 lemon
* A pinch of chili flakes (optional)
* 5 oz (140 g) smoked salmon
* 1/4 cucumber, thinly sliced
* 1 tbsp capers, drained (optional)
* 1 tbsp chopped fresh dill or chives
* Black pepper
* Lemon wedges, to serve

### Method

1. Toast the bread.
2. Meanwhile, halve and pit the avocado, scoop it into a bowl and mash with the lemon juice, a pinch of black pepper and chili flakes (if using).
3. Spread the avocado over the toast.
4. Top with the cucumber slices and smoked salmon.
5. Scatter over the capers and fresh herbs, add a twist of black pepper and serve with lemon wedges.

:::swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use a seeded gluten-free bread.
- **No fish:** top with a jammy boiled egg, smoked tofu or a spoonful of white beans mashed with lemon.
- **Budget swap:** use canned salmon or sardines mashed with lemon.
- **Salt watch:** smoked salmon is quite salty, so there is no need to add extra salt.
:::

:::why
Salmon is one of the best sources of omega-3 fats and also provides vitamin D and protein. Avocado adds healthy fats and fiber, and rye bread offers steadier energy than white bread.
:::

@recipe Ginger Brown Rice Congee with Soft Egg
@tags GF | DF | V | Make-Ahead
@meta Serves 4 | Prep 10 min | Cook 45 min | Keeps 4 days | Protein ≈25 g
Congee is the bowl that people across East Asia turn to when they need comfort, and on a morning when your stomach feels unsettled or you've slept badly, nothing soothes quite like it. Brown rice cooks with ginger and garlic until it collapses into a silky porridge; a pressure cooker gets you there in under an hour. Then cubes of tofu are stirred through, and each bowl is crowned with a jammy egg, scallions and a few drops of sesame oil. Make a big pot; it keeps for days.

### Ingredients

* 3/4 cup (150 g) brown rice, rinsed
* 6 cups (1.4 liters) low-sodium gluten-free vegetable stock or water, plus extra as needed
* A thumb-sized piece (2 in / 5 cm) of fresh ginger, peeled and cut into thin matchsticks
* 2 garlic cloves, lightly crushed
* 1 cup (70 g) shiitake or other mushrooms, sliced
* 2 cups (140 g) chopped bok choy or spinach
* 14 oz (400 g) firm tofu, cut into small cubes
* 4 eggs
* 2 tsp gluten-free tamari
* 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
* 2 scallions, thinly sliced
* 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
* Sliced fresh chili or a gluten-free chili crisp, to serve (optional; many chili crisps contain soy sauce made with wheat)

### Method

1. Put the rice, stock, ginger and garlic in a pressure cooker or multicooker. Lock the lid and cook on high pressure (or the porridge setting) for 30 minutes, then let the pressure release naturally for 10 minutes. Open carefully and stir: the rice should have broken down into a thick, creamy porridge. Add more stock or water if it's too thick.
2. Remove the garlic. Set the cooker to sauté (or transfer to a saucepan over medium heat), stir in the mushrooms and tofu and simmer for 5 minutes, then add the bok choy and cook for 2 to 3 minutes more, until just tender.
3. Meanwhile, bring a small pan of water to a boil. Gently lower in the eggs and cook for 6 1/2 minutes for soft, jammy yolks. Transfer to cold water, then peel.
4. Season the congee with the tamari. Ladle into bowls and top each with a halved egg, a few drops of sesame oil, scallions and sesame seeds. Add chili if you like.

:::swaps
- **On the stovetop:** simmer the rice, 7 cups (1.7 liters) stock, ginger and garlic in a large covered saucepan for 60 to 70 minutes, stirring every 10 to 15 minutes. It's a lovely weekend batch.
- **Using leftover rice:** simmer 2 1/2 cups (450 g) cooked brown rice with 4 cups (1 liter) stock for 20 to 25 minutes, mashing it as it cooks.
- **Swap the tofu:** use shredded leftover chicken instead, added with the mushrooms.
- **Vegan:** leave out the eggs and add an extra 7 oz (200 g) tofu.
- **Soy sauce:** if you don't need it gluten-free, regular low-sodium soy sauce works too.
- **Make ahead:** congee thickens as it cools. Reheat with a splash of water or stock, and cook the eggs fresh.
:::

:::why
Brown rice provides fiber and B vitamins, and the soft, warm texture is easy to digest. Ginger has long been used to settle the stomach, and the eggs add protein and vitamin B12.
:::
